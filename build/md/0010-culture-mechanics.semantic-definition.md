# Culture mechanics

**culture mechanics** *noun*  
**Status:** proposed theoretical term  
**Field:** moral psychology (psychology of morality), social psychology, cultural psychology, organizational psychology, organization studies, sociology of morality, sociology of culture, cultural theory, distributed cognition

## Short definition

**Culture mechanics** is a proposed framework for explaining how culture is installed in people, activated in particular situations, synchronized across groups, reproduced through institutions, and deliberately or accidentally changed.

It describes the human being as **homo duplex**, a creature with two functionally distinct processing units. The first is a powerful, general-purpose processor: the brain's main CPU. The second is a simpler social and affective processor called the **bigOther**. The bigOther does not reason in detail. It classifies thoughts, persons, actions, and situations by attaching culturally learned sentiments to them: honour or shame, loyalty or betrayal, sacredness or pollution, legitimacy or illegitimacy, belonging or exclusion.

Although the bigOther is comparatively primitive, it is connected at a decisive point. By changing how a person *feels* about what the main processor is thinking, it can redirect attention, motivation, judgment, and action before conscious reasoning notices what has happened. It is programmed slowly with a **mythological lens** and activated quickly by social signals. In terminology adapted from Systems Leadership, a **mythology** is one socially evaluated narrative or story. Many mythologies, reinforced by symbols, rituals, institutional practices, rewards, punishments, and exemplary behaviour, shape the lens through which the bigOther maps real-world observations and events onto culturally learned values and feelings.

The **single-slot principle** proposes one local bigOther executing one operative
mythological lens. That lens may braid several compatible cultural packages into a
complex program; it is not a hierarchy of parallel bigOthers. An incompatible
governing worldview must be rejected, or the incumbent weave must be unbraided
sufficiently for one successor to be rebraided and installed.

Stable installation means **complete slot occupancy** or, in a neutral
architectural sense, **full capture**. A first-order program and a lifted
second-order meta-program can each fully occupy the one slot. A lifted program does
not govern less. The order-level distinction concerns what is shared:
first-order mechanical solidarity shares concrete rules and answers; lifted
mechanical solidarity shares the rules that generate, select, apply, and revise
concrete answers. Plural lives and bounded subordinate jurisdictions can follow
from that architecture, but do not define it. Whether either form of full capture
is open and corrigible or closed and pathological is a separate question.

The bigOther also has a special capacity: it can couple with the bigOthers of other people. It encodes its classifications in words, stories, gestures, roles, titles, symbols, and sanctions that are intelligible within a shared symbolic order. Reciprocal signalling can synchronize many locally programmed individuals into an emergent social field. That field can present the collective in self-like form and, under sufficiently integrated conditions, become an **operative multi-mind-self**. Culture is the relatively stable evaluative programming and social coordination produced and reproduced through this loop.

An operative multi-mind-self need not erase the persons through whom it acts. Culture
mechanics therefore distinguishes **differentiated unity**, in which a real *we*
preserves different functions, judgments, relationships, and legitimate
jurisdictions, from **subjecthood absorption (fusion)**, in which those personal
standings are increasingly denied against the collective. Collective agency,
synchronization, and fusion are related but independent properties.

For explanatory convenience, *multi-mind-self* may also be used as a broad
family-level shorthand for a collective represented or encountered as one self.
Strict analytical claims distinguish **multi-mind-self presentation** from the
**operative multi-mind-self / multi-mind-self proper** and from a **fused
multi-mind-self**. The shorthand alone licenses no inference to intention,
ownership, responsibility, or fusion.

Some ideological symbols are **conformity signals**: their performance, absence,
or refusal makes a person's relation to the installed program publicly legible.
Such signals can turn private classification into monitoring, apparent consensus,
recruitment, moralized exclusion, institutional coercion, or collective action.
A conformity signal can also become a **targeting signal** when nonperformance
exposes a sanctionable outsider; imposed marks and attributed labels can make the
same target classification legible without requiring refusal.

In one sentence:

> **Culture mechanics is the study of how mythologies shape the mythological lens, how the bigOther uses that lens to give social feeling to observations and thoughts, and how networked bigOthers turn those feelings into collective order and action.**

## Long definition

### 1. General meaning

Culture mechanics is an explanatory model of culture as a **distributed system of sentiment classification, social coordination, and symbolic reality-construction**.

The term *mechanics* expresses a specific ambition. Culture mechanics does not stop at naming or classifying phenomena such as conformity, indoctrination, collective effervescence, moral commitment, ideology, group identity, or crowd behaviour. It asks how these phenomena are produced:

- What are the functional components?
- What information or programming does each component contain?
- How is that programming learned?
- Which signals activate it?
- How does it influence thought and action?
- How are separate people coordinated or synchronized?
- How is the resulting culture stabilized by rituals and institutions?
- Under what conditions can it be inspected, resisted, or reprogrammed?

Culture mechanics therefore treats culture not merely as a collection of customs or beliefs, but as an operative system with inputs, internal state, evaluative rules, affective outputs, communication channels, feedback loops, and institutional memory. This entry begins with the individual source and coupling interface. The emergent field and its back-action upon epistemic judgment are developed in [*Epistemic Agency under Mechanical Solidarity*](0020-epistemic-agency.semantic-definition.md).

### 2. Homo duplex: the dual-processing human

The model calls the human being **homo duplex** because human conduct results from the interaction of two functionally different processing units.

This is a deliberate extension of Émile Durkheim's term rather than a claim that Durkheim described precisely this architecture. In culture mechanics, the duality is between:

1. a powerful general-purpose processor; and
2. a simpler, culturally programmable sentiment-classification processor.

The two units are connected but not reducible to one another within this theory. This is a constitutive functional commitment, not a claim that two anatomical organs have been discovered. The bigOther must therefore not be silently redescribed as merely System 1, a generic heuristic, the superego, internalized norms, or an output of ordinary cognition. Those concepts may illuminate aspects of its operation, but none replaces the proposed second component without revising the theory itself.

A person's intelligence, knowledge, or technical competence does not by itself reveal how the person's bigOther is programmed. The main CPU may remain intelligent, computationally powerful, and capable of elementary verification while the bigOther changes which conclusions feel admissible, honourable, shameful, safe, or unthinkable. Conversely, a person's cultural programming does not determine the full extent of the person's intellectual capacities.

This relative independence explains why a highly intelligent person may be strongly governed by ideological or religious classifications, while a person of ordinary intelligence may preserve considerable moral and epistemic independence. The main processor determines much of what a person *can* think; the bigOther strongly influences which thoughts feel admirable, shameful, safe, dangerous, permissible, or unthinkable.

### 3. First processing unit: the main CPU

The **main CPU** is the brain's powerful, flexible, general-purpose processing capacity. Its functions include:

- perception and memory;
- language and explicit thought;
- comparison and inference;
- imagination and counterfactual reasoning;
- planning and problem-solving;
- conscious deliberation;
- personal judgment;
- construction of explanations.

Most ordinary conceptions of intelligence focus on this processor. It can learn complex theories, solve technical problems, interpret evidence, and devise sophisticated strategies.

The main CPU does not, however, choose its direction in a cultural vacuum. Attention, motivation, felt relevance, moral urgency, and the acceptability of possible conclusions are influenced by the second processor.

### 4. Second processing unit: the bigOther

The **bigOther** is the comparatively primitive but strategically placed
sentiment-classification processor. Culture mechanics distinguishes its functional
operation, its phenomenological appearance, and the shared symbolic interface
through which it couples with other local bigOthers.

#### Functional aspect

The local bigOther executes the installed mythological lens. It does not usually
produce a detailed argument. It assigns social and moral valence to observations,
thoughts, persons, sources, roles, actions, symbols, and events:

```text
observation, thought, person, source, role, action, symbol, or event
                              ↓
                    MYTHOLOGICAL LENS
            learned value-and-feeling mappings
                              ↓
                         BIGOTHER
                 fast sentiment classification
                              ↓
honour / shame          loyalty / betrayal       sacred / profane
clean / polluted        legitimate / illicit     safe / threatening
admirable / contemptible                           belonging / exclusion
```

This mapping function corresponds to what **Systems Leadership** calls the
**mythological lens**. Culture mechanics treats the lens as the installed
evaluative program and the local bigOther as the comparatively simple processor
that executes it. Its output can take the form of pride, guilt, shame, disgust,
trust, fear, admiration, moral elevation, indignation, belonging, or anticipated
exclusion. These outputs alter which thoughts receive attention, which
possibilities are emotionally tolerable, and which actions feel obligatory.

#### Phenomenological aspect

The source of a classification need not be experienced as “my learned social
programming.” It may be encountered as an actually present Other—God, Destiny,
Nation, History, the People, or the Cause—that sees, judges, addresses, commands,
and can receive loyalty, service, protection, or sacrifice. Culture mechanics
calls the felt objectivity of this encounter **bigOther-presence** and its
second-person relational form **bigOther-as-Thou**.

When the installed program is jealous, this presence may itself be felt directly
as jealous: as a *Thou* that claims the person, watches for disloyalty, refuses
divided allegiance, and demands fidelity against rivals. The person need not
experience this as “my program classified an alternative attachment as betrayal.”
It can be lived as “the Other is jealous and requires my loyalty.”

[William James's *The Varieties of Religious Experience*
(1902)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/621/621-h/621-h.html) supplies an important
phenomenological antecedent: persons can experience an unseen order or objective
presence as more authoritative and conduct-directing than an explicit proposition.
[Martin Buber's *I and Thou*
(1923)](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/I-And-Thou/Martin-Buber/9780684717258)
supplies the relational grammar of encountering another as a *Thou* rather than an
object or *It*. Neither thinker proposed the culture-mechanical processor or its
distributed field. Buber's eternal Thou in particular must not be reduced to a
socially programmed classifier. The present terms borrow aspects of their
phenomenology while assigning them a different functional explanation.

Kant's account of conscience supplies a further moral-phenomenological neighbour.
In [*The Metaphysics of Morals*, AA
6:438](https://korpora.org/kant/aa06/438.html) and [AA
6:439](https://www.korpora.org/Kant/aa06/439.html), one person conducts an inner
court case while experiencing its judge as an observing and commanding other.
Kant's **doubled personality** (*zwiefache Persönlichkeit*) is not the
culture-mechanical homo-duplex architecture: Kant explains the division through
autonomous practical reason, whereas Culture Mechanics proposes a culturally
programmed local bigOther. The controlled comparison is developed below in
[“Kant's inner tribunal and doubled
personality”](#kants-inner-tribunal-and-doubled-personality).

This account also leaves the metaphysical status of a reported presence open. Its
claim is narrower: whatever else the presence may be, the person's local bigOther
can mediate how it becomes socially classified and conduct-directing.

When the mythology represents an agent, several depths of relation and enactment
must be distinguished:

| Depth | Culture-mechanical description | Relation to fusion |
|---|---|---|
| **Representation** | “This culture tells stories about God, a god, the Nation, History, the People, or the Cause.” | No fusion follows from representation. |
| **Consultation or service** | The person asks what the represented agent would judge or accepts obligations toward it. | Personal authorship can remain fully legitimate. |
| **bigOther-as-Thou** | The agent is experienced as present, addressing, watching, judging, or commanding the person. | An *I--Thou* relation still contains two poles; presence is not fusion by itself. |
| **Distributed enactment** | Several persons and institutions provide perception, memory, judgment, voice, resources, and action to the represented agent. | Enactment can remain differentiated or become absorptive. |
| **Fused enactment** | The collectively enacted agent becomes the authoritative subject and the person's independent authorship loses standing against it. | This is subjecthood absorption by definition. |

The sequence is analytical rather than a necessary developmental ladder. A person
can serve without reporting a presence, report a presence without joining a
collective agent, or participate in collective enactment while retaining a
legitimate conscience, judgment, and right of refusal. Conversely, an intensely
agentified programme can make “the Other speaks and acts through me” the operative
locus of authorship.

The phrase **agent-model** in this comparison means the culturally specified
representation by which people anticipate what an agent perceives, remembers,
wants, judges, and commands. It is part of the operative mythological lens; it is
not another local bigOther. Nor does analysis of a humanly implemented God-model
decide whether God exists independently of that implementation. Culture Mechanics
can examine the representation, experience, and social enactment while leaving
the theological or metaphysical referent open.

#### Shared symbolic protocol

The spelling **bigOther** marks a deliberate adaptation of [Jacques Lacan's *big
Other*](https://www.seuil.com/ouvrage/le-seminaire-livre-xvii-jacques-lacan/9782020130448),
the locus of language, law, roles, conventions, authority, and socially
intelligible meaning. Culture mechanics does not identify Lacan's symbolic order
with a processor inside the individual. It treats that shared order, in one
functional translation, as the **on-the-wire protocol** through which local
bigOthers become mutually legible:

```text
state of A's local bigOther
              ↓ encoded as
words, stories, gestures, roles, titles, symbols, sanctions
              ↓ transmitted through
shared symbolic order / Lacanian big Other
              ↓ interpreted through
B's installed mythological lens
              ↓
B's sentiment classification and outward response
```

An alphabet is not a message, and Unicode or ASCII is not the state of the
computer using it. They provide shared encodings through which different machines
can exchange intelligible signals. Likewise, a letter such as `M` can denote a
complete mathematical mapping, while `E` or `B` can denote an entire field. Flags,
uniforms, titles, and words such as *betrayal* or *justice* can function as
similarly compressed social tokens because participants have learned how to
unpack them.

The protocol transmits public signals, not perfect copies of private states.
Synchronization requires sufficiently compatible lenses and contexts; it does not
require identical inner contents. A signal can therefore activate convergence,
misunderstanding, indifference, or opposition.

The bigOther's power comes less from computational sophistication than from its
**position in the control architecture**. It influences how a person feels about
the main CPU's thoughts. The main CPU may subsequently use its much greater power
to explain, implement, defend, or occasionally question the direction supplied by
the bigOther.

#### Historical cause, live classification, and avowed reason

A present cultural response can contain three causally distinct layers:

1. **Historical cause:** the remote social experience, institutional arrangement,
   selection process, conflict, or practical problem through which a rule and its
   affective weight arose.
2. **Live classification:** the attraction, horror, shame, sacredness, loyalty, or
   other sentiment currently executed when the operative lens encounters the
   object.
3. **Avowed reason:** the explanation a person or institution can presently state
   for that response.

These layers can coincide, but they need not. The original problem can disappear
while an affectively alive prohibition remains; a later theology, science, or
political doctrine can then supply a new explanation. Conversely, a person can
repeat an inherited reason after its corresponding classification has become
affectively dead.

Émile Durkheim supplies a particularly clear observation in [“Les principes de
1789 et la
sociologie”](https://education.persee.fr/doc/revin_1775-6014_1890_num_19_1_9561_t1_0450_0000_1)
(1890; Mark Traugott's English translation appears in [*Émile Durkheim on
Morality and
Society*](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/E/bo3627098.html),
pp. 34–42). He writes that conscious prescriptions can be “the conscious outcome
of an entire unconscious process” and that, without access to their remote causes,
“we invent reasons for them, lacking the real ones which we do not see.” His
kin-marriage example separates a surviving horror from the religious or
physiological explanations later offered for it.

This is testimony to the **phenomenon to be explained**, not proof of the present
processor architecture. Implicit learning, habit, affective conditioning,
predictive processing, strategic rationalization, and other unitary-mind accounts
remain alternatives. A discriminating study should therefore vary or measure the
three layers separately: alter an explicit reason while holding the social
approval environment roughly constant; alter approval while holding the
proposition constant; test whether affective reversal precedes or follows
doctrinal revision; and compare people who avow the same reason but display
different classifications.

### 5. The single-slot principle

Culture mechanics proposes that the bigOther occupies the equivalent of a
computer's **single PCI slot**. There is one slot, one installed local bigOther,
and one operative mythological lens at a time. The bigOther is the processor or
card; the mythological lens is the executable evaluative program it runs.

A stable program produces **complete slot occupancy**. This document also calls
that architectural condition **full capture**. Here *full* means that one operative
weave governs the slot, not that the weave is false, totalitarian, or
uncorrectable. A **first-order full capture** shares binding concrete rules and
answers. A **lifted full capture** shares binding rules that generate, select,
apply, and revise concrete answers:

> **fixed meta-rules M + current knowledge or inputs K_t + situation C → binding
> first-order output R_t(C)**

Here *fixed* means installed and binding at a given time, not eternal. Generator
and outputs belong to the same operative weave. Both architectures occupy the slot
completely; lifting changes the order at which binding sameness is located rather
than producing an empty or half-programmed slot.

This neutral architectural **full capture** must not be confused with
**Culture Program capture**, in which the authorities, metrics, appointments,
correction routes, and reproduction machinery of an incumbent program are
redirected until they install or reproduce an incompatible successor. A healthy
lifted meta-order and a closed first-order program can each be full captures
without either having captured a predecessor in that ecological sense. The
successor-displacement construct and its evidence protocol are defined in
[*Culture Program Ecology*](0060-culture-program-ecology.semantic-definition.md#112-culture-program-capture).

Four axes must therefore remain distinct: **slot occupancy** (full), **order
level** (shared outputs or shared generator), **jurisdiction** (what the installed
rules govern), and **corrigibility** (open or closed). A lifted meta-order can have
universal jurisdiction at its own level over the creation, allocation, limitation,
coordination, succession, and correction of first-order outputs and jurisdictions.
Persons, offices, professions, institutions, communities, and contracting parties
may remain plural and domain-limited beneath it. That pluralism is an important
possible result of lifting, not its definition.

This **single-slot exclusivity** does not imply that the installed program is
simple, culturally pure, or derived from only one source. A person may belong to
several groups and encounter many religions, professions, political doctrines,
familial obligations, and moral traditions. Compatible material can be assembled
like software packages or libraries and braided into one operative lens. The slot
restricts the number of independently operative evaluators, not the complexity or
modularity of the installed weave.

Compatible packages are not separate bigOthers waiting for a higher process to
rank them at runtime. Their objects, authorities, roles, and permissions already
receive meaning through the one combined weave. **Paul's pride in supporting
himself through work illustrates the point: Christianity and a work ethic need not
compete for supremacy.** In [1 Corinthians 9:6–18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Corinthians%209%3A6-18),
especially verses 15–18, Paul connects his boast with preaching without making use
of his right to material support; [1 Thessalonians 2:9](https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/1%20Thessalonians%202%3A9)
and [2 Thessalonians 3:7–10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Thessalonians%203%3A7-10)
present labouring so as not to burden the community as an exemplary practice. A
Christian mythology package and a *Berufsethos* can therefore reinforce one
another within a single program. This is an illustration of compatibility, not
population-level proof that every religious and professional package is compatible.

New cultural material consequently has three broad fates:

1. **Incumbent assimilation or rejection:** the installed lens interprets,
   neutralizes, reuses, or refuses the material in its own terms without surrendering
   its load-bearing classifications.
2. **Compatible integration:** a package whose important classifications and
   authority relations can coexist with the incumbent is braided into the one
   operative lens.
3. **Replacement under incompatibility:** a rival governing program cannot be
   installed beside the incumbent. The incumbent weave must be loosened and
   unbraided sufficiently for retained strands and new material to be rebraided into
   one successor lens.

Within the originating application developed here, **Christianity and the National
Socialist worldview are deeply incompatible programming systems** at load-bearing
classifications such as God and ultimate authority, the person and neighbour, race
and nation, truth, obedience, sacrifice, violence, and loyalty. Nazi programming
could not simply be installed beside an intact Christian program. Capture required
a replacement process. A Nazi-governed successor could retain Christian words,
scripture, symbols, rituals, offices, or a subjective Christian identity, but those
strands would receive their operative meaning from the successor weave. The result
would be one rebraided Nazi-Christian program, not two programs ordered by rank.

Deep cultural change is therefore unlike adding neutral information to memory.
Reprogramming changes the weave that determines what new information means and how
it feels. Incomplete replacement can produce contradiction, instability, or a
tangled lens during loosening and rebraiding; it does not establish several
independent bigOthers running in parallel. This helps explain why conversion,
deradicalization, psychoanalysis, moral injury, ideological rupture, and profound
changes of identity can be slow, conflictual, and emotionally painful.

#### Hard cases and possible defeat conditions

Single-slot exclusivity cannot be protected merely by redescribing every apparent
counterexample as “one complicated braid.” Self-description, retained vocabulary,
public ritual, or several group memberships do not decide the architecture. The
diagnostic question is:

> **When inherited authorities conflict over the same act, which rule or authority
> has final interpretive priority over what the retained material means?**

The following observations remain consistent with one slot:

- one tradition interprets the other's retained symbols and practices;
- outward conformity is strategic camouflage under legal or social coercion;
- selected strands have been integrated into one syncretic successor;
- a third rule governs how both inheritances are combined;
- or the person is in unstable, incomplete unbraiding and rebraiding.

A strong candidate counterexample requires substantially more:

1. two governing evaluators issue incompatible classifications in the same domain;
2. each remains independently final rather than being interpreted, authorized, or
   limited by the other or by a third rule;
3. each can generate its own affect, guilt, correction, and veto;
4. neither is merely reflective knowledge, role performance, strategic concealment,
   or a de-animated remnant; and
5. the configuration remains stable over time rather than resolving into
   hierarchy, integration, replacement, or breakdown.

Evidence of that pattern would count against strict single-slot exclusivity. The
theory would then need either a principled account of parallel operative
evaluators or revision of the one-slot commitment. Evidence for only labels,
rituals, memories, or alternating contexts is insufficient because one weave can
assign different outputs to different contexts. The detailed dual-religious-
identification boundary case belongs to [*Epistemic Agency under Mechanical
Solidarity*, “Examples, cases, and boundary
tests”](0020-epistemic-agency.semantic-definition.md#dual-religious-identification-and-the-converso-hard-case).

### 6. Systems Leadership: mythologies and the mythological lens

[*Systems Leadership: Creating Positive
Organisations*](https://www.routledge.com/Systems-Leadership-Creating-Positive-Organisations/MacDonald-Burke-Stewart/p/book/9781138036574),
by Ian Macdonald, Catherine Burke, and Karl Stewart, supplies the definitive
practical account used by Culture Mechanics for how culture is deliberately
implemented. Its method coordinates leadership behaviour, systems, symbols,
mythologies, habits, organizational arrangements, and repeated experience rather
than treating mythology as sufficient by itself. Culture Mechanics embeds that
organizational and workplace account in a larger theory and develops selected
extensions and refinements; readers seeking the exact implementation method should
read the book.

> **Culture Mechanics condenses the Systems Leadership implementation stack into
> one memorable duality: systems are the skeleton; human carriers, mythology,
> meaning, affect, *Beseelung*, and embodied habits are the flesh and blood. The
> skeleton makes culture durable and repeatable; the living body makes it
> operative and alive.**

The full semantic anatomy and its boundaries belong to [*Culture Program
Ecology*](0060-culture-program-ecology.semantic-definition.md#137-systemic-skeleton-and-living-cultural-body).

Culture mechanics adopts the following terminological hierarchy:

| Term | Definition in culture mechanics |
|---|---|
| **Mythology** | One socially evaluated story or narrative unit. It need not involve gods or supernatural events. It shows persons, roles, actions, conflicts, and consequences while indicating what deserves admiration, shame, loyalty, fear, trust, disgust, or condemnation. |
| **Mythologies** | The accumulated and partly interconnected body of such stories available to a person or group. They provide many examples of how the social world is supposed to be interpreted. |
| **Mythological lens** | The generalized mapping formed from those mythologies and reinforced by systems, symbols, rituals, sanctions, and observed conduct. It maps real-world observations and events onto culturally learned values and feelings. |
| **bigOther** | The local sentiment-classification processor that executes the installed mythological lens and applies its mappings quickly. |

The lens is one executable weave but may be modular. Mythologies, practices,
professional ethics, familial duties, and local customs can operate as mutually
compatible packages within it. Their plurality is a plurality of constituent
strands and context-sensitive mappings, not a plurality of local bigOthers.

This yields the central culture-mechanical sequence:

```text
MYTHOLOGY
one evaluated story
                    ↓
MYTHOLOGIES
many mutually reinforcing stories
                    ↓
MYTHOLOGICAL LENS
generalized mappings from observations to values and feelings
                    ↓
BIGOTHER
fast execution of those mappings
                    ↓
ELEPHANT AND MAIN CPU
felt attraction, aversion, obligation, attention, and motivation
```

An individual mythology can teach:

- which kinds of persons and roles exist;
- what counts as a good group member;
- which authorities are legitimate;
- which conduct receives praise or condemnation;
- which sacrifices are honourable;
- what constitutes betrayal, pollution, injustice, or redemption;
- how the past explains the present;
- what the group expects of the individual;
- which future is desirable or disastrous.

A story acts as an evaluated simulation. Narrative transportation places the listener inside a conflict; a hero or villain demonstrates a possible response; consequences display what follows; praise or condemnation supplies the sentiment label; and the audience's reaction shows whether the classification is collectively shared. Each story is one training example. Many convergent stories allow the bigOther to generalize a lens that can classify new situations never described in any one story.

#### Relevant structures and moral appraisal

Dushan Wegner's *Relevante Strukturen* (2019; ISBN 9783739405636) calls attention
to the relations in which a person is embedded and to the larger structures that
the person experiences as important. Culture Mechanics adopts **relevant
structure** as a typed relational object rather than as a synonym for anything a
person happens to value:

> A **relevant structure** is a represented relational arrangement or organized
> condition--such as a family, friendship, profession, institution, community,
> legal order, practice, person-in-relation, or anticipated shared future--that
> the installed mythological lens treats as carrying moral weight.

Not every desired object, isolated preference, or momentarily salient stimulus is
therefore a relevant structure. The term emphasizes embedding, dependence,
continuity, function, and vulnerability. A mythology can teach which structures
exist, who or what belongs within them, how they depend upon one another, what
counts as supporting or damaging them, whose claims have precedence when they
conflict, and who has standing or jurisdiction to judge and act.

Wegner's memorable formulation that good strengthens relevant structures and evil
weakens them is incorporated as the **relevant-structure appraisal hypothesis**:

> **People often experience an action, omission, continuation, or proposed change
> as good when they believe it strengthens, protects, repairs, or honourably
> serves a structure they experience as relevant, and as evil when they believe
> it weakens, corrupts, betrays, or destroys such a structure.**

This is a descriptive hypothesis about moral perception and mobilization, not a
sufficient definition of moral truth and not a claim that every moral judgment
uses this route. The analysis must keep five variables distinct:

| Variable | Question |
|---|---|
| **Felt relevance** | How much moral weight does this person or operative culture assign to the represented structure? |
| **Represented effect** | What does the person believe the action will do to it? |
| **Actual effect** | What does the action do, including indirect, delayed, and transferred effects? |
| **Generative centrality** | How strongly does the structure actually produce capabilities or conditions upon which several downstream structures depend? |
| **Normative standing** | Is the structure, claimed effect, jurisdiction, or proposed sacrifice legitimate? |

The [Hanekamp--Wegner ecological
application](0040-mythology-construction.semantic-definition.md#change-deterioration-moral-appraisal)
sharpens the change boundary: **“Deterioration is a moral evaluation of
change.”** A measurement or model may establish or predict change, but the
classification *deterioration* additionally selects a reference state, relevant
structure, represented weakening, normative standing, and interpreter. This is a
Culture Mechanics bridge from Hanekamp's application to Wegner's appraisal
hypothesis, not Wegner's own formulation or proof that the evaluation is
arbitrary.

**Generative centrality**--also described more informally as
*load-bearingness*--is therefore not another name for felt relevance. A structure
may be causally central to the production of character, knowledge, cooperation, or
institutional continuity while receiving little explicit moral attention.
Conversely, an intensely felt-relevant structure may be weakly generative,
replaceable, or destructive. A **cultural sublimation architecture** is a
particularly important proposed case: it can recurrently produce capacities upon
which families, professions, law, science, markets, and political institutions
depend even when the operative culture does not represent that architecture
itself as relevant. The concept and its limits are developed in [*Mythology
Construction*](0040-mythology-construction.semantic-definition.md), “Sublimation
and moral formation.”

That document also distinguishes the larger architecture from a
[**lifted sublimation
generator**](0040-mythology-construction.semantic-definition.md#the-lifted-sublimation-generator-pattern):
installed meta-rules select binding directions, affective actuation makes them
motivationally effective, and formative practices turn them into durable
character and capability. Generator efficacy does not by itself establish
positive normative valence.

The mismatch is analytically consequential:

```text
high generative centrality
+ low felt relevance
→ weak protection and transmission
→ vulnerability to neglect, ridicule, dismantling,
  or consumption of inherited cultural capital
```

This is a hypothesis about dependency and reproduction, not a presumption that
every inherited arrangement is beneficial or that whatever calls itself
civilizational must be preserved.

Wegner's emphasis on *Veränderung* also motivates the more limited
**change-indexed moral-mobilization hypothesis**:

> **Moral classification often becomes especially salient and action-directing
> when a condition is represented as causing, accelerating, continuing,
> preventing, or culpably failing to prevent a change in a felt-relevant
> structure.**

This concerns represented counterfactual movement, not physical novelty. “The
rule exists” can be rendered as “officials continue the rule,” “participants
decline to end it,” or “the institution prevents transition to an alternative.”
Conversely, the absence of visible mobilization does not establish that the status
quo is experienced as good: coercion, habituation, helplessness, low salience,
concealed dissent, and the absence of a represented alternative are competing
explanations. A direct test would hold material conditions constant while comparing
a static-state description, an active-continuation description, and a
prevented-alternative description. Failure of these descriptions to alter moral
intensity or action readiness, especially across independently measured relevance,
would weaken the hypothesis.

The mythological lens need not calculate complex consequences by itself. Ordinary
cognition, testimony, institutions, and a mythology's causal stories can supply a
represented effect model; the local bigOther then rapidly classifies the
represented support or damage through the installed lens:

```text
observed, proposed, continued, or prevented change
                         ↓
main CPU + received causal stories represent likely effects
                         ↓
operative lens supplies felt relevance, moral meaning,
precedence, standing, and jurisdiction
                         ↓
local bigOther classifies protection/injury, loyalty/betrayal,
repair/destruction, justice/injustice, honour/shame
                         ↓
attention, judgment, motivation, and action are redirected
```

“Strengthening” and “weakening” are multidimensional rather than one moral scalar.
An oppressive institution may increase its continuity, capacity, reach, or internal
cohesion while destroying truth-contact, corrigibility, personal standing, or
legitimate exit and while transferring severe costs to other persons and
structures. The appraisal hypothesis explains why participants may nevertheless
experience its preservation as good; it does not vindicate that appraisal.

Several relevant structures can coexist within the one operative weave without
implying several local bigOthers. Their relative weight, conflict rules, and
jurisdictions are program content. A predominantly first-order solidarity may make
a concrete hierarchy of structures binding as such. A lifted solidarity may make
binding a procedure by which competing claims are evidenced, limited, adjudicated,
appealed, and revised. This is an application of the order-level distinction, not
its definition; in both cases the one slot remains fully occupied.

Systems Leadership adds an important qualification: explicit stories are not the only influences on the lens. The conduct of leaders, the design of systems, the use of symbols, institutional rewards and sanctions, and repeated experiences of belonging, success, humiliation, or exclusion can confirm or contradict the lessons conveyed by a mythology. These experiences may themselves later be narrated as new mythologies.

[*Culture Program Ecology*](0060-culture-program-ecology.semantic-definition.md#137-systemic-skeleton-and-living-cultural-body)
formalizes the resulting implementation anatomy as a **systemic skeleton** joined
to a **living cultural body**. Rules, offices, jurisdictions, incentives,
feedback, accounting, correction, and succession make the program repeatable;
human carriers, mythologies, meaning, sacredness, affect, moral formation,
*Beseelung*, and embodied habits animate and interpret it. These are two aspects
of one operative Culture Program, not two bigOthers or a new processor division.

The relationship is therefore not simply “a story causes a feeling.” Rather:

```text
stories + observed conduct + systems + symbols + repeated experience
                              ↓
              reinforce or contradict mythologies
                              ↓
                  shape the mythological lens
                              ↓
       lens maps a new observation onto value and feeling
```

Systems Leadership commonly describes the lens as evaluating behaviour, systems, and symbols along continua such as:

- honesty–dishonesty;
- trust–distrust;
- courage–cowardice;
- respect–disrespect for human dignity;
- fairness–unfairness;
- love or care–indifference.

Culture mechanics interprets these as important output dimensions of the programmed bigOther. They are not necessarily an exhaustive list of human moral sentiments, but they show concretely what the lens does: it converts a physical or social observation into an affectively consequential classification.

When people possess sufficiently similar mythological lenses, they tend to interpret the same conduct in similar ways. Systems Leadership calls the resulting shared evaluative pattern culture. Culture mechanics adds that compatible local bigOthers can also exchange signals and synchronize, turning shared classification into distributed affect and coordinated action.

### 7. Programming, activation, and reprogramming

Culture mechanics distinguishes several operations that are easily confused:

| Operation | Timescale | Function |
|---|---:|---|
| **Programming** | Slow | Adds, reinforces, or revises mythologies and compatible packages and thereby installs, extends, or modifies the one mythological lens used by the bigOther. |
| **Classification** | Very fast | The bigOther applies the installed lens to a person, thought, action, symbol, or event. |
| **Activation** | Fast | A present signal activates an already learned mapping in the lens. |
| **Synchronization** | Fast | Reciprocal signals align the affective or behavioural states of several people. |
| **Reinforcement** | Repeated | Successful, praised, punished, or emotionally intense execution stabilizes mythologies and their mappings in the lens. |
| **Reprogramming** | Usually slow | Modifies the operative weave. Compatible change can extend or repair it; incompatible replacement requires sufficient unbraiding of the incumbent and rebraiding of one successor lens. |
| **Unbraiding and rebraiding** | Usually slow and conflictual | Loosens load-bearing bindings, preserves or detaches selected strands, crosses a potentially unstable interval, and installs one successor weave rather than a parallel evaluator. |

#### Situated cultural scripts: executable local braids

A **situated cultural script** is a distributed, role-, institution-, or
situation-indexed braid that translates selected meanings, classifications,
permissions, and authority relations of an operative cultural weave into expected
perception, conduct, coordination, improvisation, and access to practical,
relational, or material support. It is narrower in scope but more concretely
specified than the general cultural braid.

The abstraction relation is:

| Level | Culture-mechanical meaning |
|---|---|
| **Operative cultural braid** | The comparatively general organization of meanings, values, identities, authorities, and conflict rules within the one installed weave |
| **Situated cultural script** | A higher-resolution adapter specifying how selected parts of that order operate for people in a recurrent role, institution, relationship, or situation |
| **Local script variant** | A historically or organizationally particular implementation of the more general script |
| **Enactment** | One concrete performance, modification, improvisation, or refusal of the script by actual participants |

```text
operative cultural weave C
+ institution or relationship I
+ role R
+ recurrent situation X
+ practical and material environment E
→ situated script S(C,I,R,X,E)
→ expected perception, conduct, coordination, and improvisation
```

The notation marks contextual specification, not deterministic execution or a
calibrated function. A professional script, for example, may join a society's
general meanings of work, service, competence, authority, and responsibility to
formal offices, tacit routines, tools, colleague expectations, error-recovery
practices, status, livelihood, and professional pride. Participants may interpret,
perform, repair, exploit, or refuse it.

A script can be larger than any individual. It may be maintained across many
persons, offices, artifacts, procedures, relationships, and resource flows, while
particular people enter and leave its roles. The script is therefore neither a
private plan nor necessarily a collective agent. It supplies a distributed
pattern for action; agency still belongs to participating persons, institutions,
or, where the independent functional threshold is met, an operative multi-mind-self.

Scripts cut across the culture-mechanical object types defined below. They can
contain program units, symbols, implementation arrangements, expected local
execution, and interfaces among several roles. They are not another processor or
another governing bigOther. One fully occupied mythological lens can support many
compatible familial, occupational, civic, religious, and technical scripts. Their
scope, priority, and conflict rules belong to the one operative weave. Stable
same-situation scripts that remain incompatibly final and mutually ungoverned
would instead raise the existing single-slot defeat condition.

The term is adapted from [Venkatesh Rao's “On the Unraveling of
Scripts”](https://ribbonfarm.com/2013/06/06/on-the-unraveling-of-scripts/).
Rao treats scripts as learned behavioural patterns that supply meaning and
material support and treats their unraveling as a mutually reinforcing loss of
both. The situated-braid definition, its placement between culture and enactment,
the single-slot boundary, and the distinctions among local repair, cross-script
cascade, and cultural unbraiding are Culture Mechanics developments.

**Script viability** asks whether the script continues to translate cultural
meaning into intelligible and practically supportable conduct under its actual
conditions. Viability is not truth, legitimacy, flourishing, corrigibility,
*Beseelung*, or moral goodness. A false or oppressive script can remain highly
viable, while a worthy script can fail for lack of skill, infrastructure,
resources, institutional support, or workable interfaces.

**Script unraveling** is the process by which a situated script loses coherence or
viability: inherited meanings cease to guide workable conduct, practical
consequences cease confirming the script, roles and procedures contradict one
another, participants improvise incompatible responses, or supporting
institutions and resources fail. Meaning-side and practical-side failure can
reinforce one another:

```text
script supplies meaning, role, and expected conduct
                    ↓
participants enact and coordinate through the script
                    ↓
practice produces relational and material consequences
                    ↓
consequences confirm, correct, or discredit the script
                    ↺

loss on either side
→ weaker enactment, coordination, support, or credibility
→ further loss on the other side
→ possible accelerating script unraveling
```

Unraveling and cultural unbraiding are coupled but non-equivalent:

```text
peripheral script unravels
→ local repair or script replacement within the incumbent cultural braid

load-bearing or mutually dependent scripts unravel
→ cross-script cascade
→ general meanings and authorities lose practical confirmation
→ possible cultural unbraiding

general cultural braid is unbraided
→ several dependent scripts lose common orientation
→ possible widespread script unraveling
```

The **load-bearingness** of a script is the extent to which the wider weave or
other scripts depend upon its successful operation and visible confirmation.
Failure of one peripheral routine therefore does not establish cultural collapse;
failure across family formation, productive work, lawful succession, public
truth-testing, or another highly connected script family may propagate much
further. The direction and extent of propagation are empirical questions.

Scriptlessness in Rao's sense must not be translated as an empty PCI slot. It
means that no viable action repertoire is available within the relevant domain.
The local slot remains fully occupied, although its installed weave may be
de-animated, tangled, closed, practically obsolete, or unable to generate an
adequate situated response. Nor is script unraveling identical to Babelization:
one widely shared script can fail while participants remain mutually intelligible,
and locally viable scripts can coexist while their interfaces fall below the
anti-Babel floor.

Applied to relevant structures, **relevance activation** foregrounds a concern and
appraisal mapping already installed in the lens. **Relevance reprogramming**
changes which relational arrangements carry moral weight, how strongly they
matter, what support or damage means, or how conflicts among them are resolved.
Repeated foregrounding may reinforce and eventually help reprogram the lens, but a
one-shot increase in salience does not by itself establish durable reprogramming.

The theory studies one endogenous formation path and one prospective purposive
research route.

**Endogenous mythology formation** is the largely distributed and not centrally
designed process through which changes in social structure, institutions, lived
practice, and receiver dispositions generate pressures, needs, and aspirations;
competing articulations are then selected, sacralized, embodied, and reproduced.
It is the ordinary cultural-growth path. *Endogenous* describes causal provenance,
not innocence, health, inevitability, or moral legitimacy.

**Purposive mythology construction** is the open research possibility that a
person, office, organization, or coalition might adopt a proposed destination and
deliberately intervene in the mythologies, causal classifications, symbols, roles,
permissions, and supporting institutions through which a population interprets
and acts. Any attempted constructor remains inside culture: the desired
destination, available materials, authority, and perception of the problem have
themselves been culturally formed, and the intervention becomes one more input
into receiver, institutional, rival, and reality selection.

The detailed semantic definition, design audit, and workflow belong to
[*Mythology Construction*](0040-mythology-construction.semantic-definition.md);
the present section states only the distinction needed by the core causal cycle.

```text
ENDOGENOUS FORMATION
changing conditions → diffuse mismatch → competing articulations
→ distributed selection → embodiment and reproduction

PROSPECTIVE PURPOSIVE CONSTRUCTION
chosen destination D*
→ diagnose receivers, incumbent order, and likely baseline trajectory
→ deliberately alter mythologies and supporting arrangements
→ prototype, observe, correct, defend, or retire
```

The prospective branch treats mythology as a privileged symbolic intervention
surface because evaluated stories encode roles, causes, values, permissions, and
consequences in a form close to the bigOther's learned operations. It is not the
whole implementation surface. Leadership behaviour, systems, symbols, habits,
rewards, sanctions, material conditions, and repeated experience may confirm,
redirect, or defeat an intended mythology. An attempted purpose therefore does not
imply control:

```text
actual successor
= intended destination × receiver fit × incumbent resistance
× institutional selection × rival intervention
× unintended consequence × reality
```

The multiplicative notation marks joint constraint, not a measured equation.

A slogan, flag, gesture, uniform, accusation, facial expression, or news event normally does not transmit a complete mythological lens. It is a small trigger interpreted by an already programmed bigOther. The same signal can therefore produce opposite reactions in people with differently formed lenses. When the event is subsequently organized and retold as an evaluated story, it can also become a new mythology that reinforces or modifies the lens.

A **master signifier** is an especially dense activation signal. In the
co-developed analogy adopted here, it is a **highly compressed protocol token**
that indexes and activates an already installed complex of stories,
classifications, authorities, identities, sentiments, and expected conduct. The
token does not contain that whole complex any more than `M` contains every ordered
pair of a mathematical mapping. Its power depends upon prior programming and a
shared convention for expanding it. Longer stories, repeated practice, and
institutional experience can change the program; the token usually calls the
program already present.

The same distinction applies when a slogan, song, hero, ritual, work, or exemplary
practice evokes a culturally shared sublimation. The carrier does not contain the
sublimation process or the durable cultural arrangement that teaches it. It
activates installed mappings connecting the represented practice to honour,
shame, capability, contribution, belonging, or obligation. A rival or cynical
mythology can activate a contrary classification; repeated stories, symbols,
rewards, sanctions, systems, and observed conduct can reinforce that mapping and
eventually reprogram the operative lens:

```text
incumbent mythologies:
sublimated practice → honour, contribution, capability
                              ↓ rival reclassification
sublimated practice → exploitation, foolishness, conformity, futility
                              ↓ repeated cultural implementation
reinforcement or reprogramming of the mythological lens
                              ↓
local bigOther supplies changed sentiment and motivation
                              ↓
changed enactment, Beseelung, and intergenerational transmission
```

Wegner's compression and decompression vocabulary supplies a useful second view
of this operation: a small token can stand in for a much larger installed complex.
In the constitutive language of Culture Mechanics, however, the intrapersonal
target is the programming of the mythological lens. A “decoder” is an analogy for
recipient reconstruction through ordinary cognition, learned context, the
installed lens, and local-bigOther classification--not a third processor.
Prestigious examples, ridicule, rewards, and sanctions are programming and
reinforcement channels rather than a substitute for the lens. *Beseelung* remains
the independent degree to which the resulting program is affectively alive and
conduct-directing, not a separate decoding faculty or necessarily a conscious
willingness.

Activation and replacement must therefore not be confused. A signal can activate
or reinforce material already compatible with the installed lens. It cannot by
itself make an incompatible governing program co-occupy the slot. Such capture
requires the incumbent's authority relations and classifications to be loosened,
detached, or reinterpreted and a successor weave to be constructed.

### 8. Culture-mechanical objects, ideological programs, conformity signals, and targeting signals

This section develops a mini-theory of the objects through which cultural programs
become personally operative and publicly legible. Its authoritative home is
*Culture Mechanics* because the mechanism begins at the interface among an
installed lens, the local bigOther that executes it, and a socially intelligible
token. The mechanism can then scale into the mechanical-solidarity field developed
in [*Epistemic Agency under Mechanical
Solidarity*](0020-epistemic-agency.semantic-definition.md). A symbol is not merely
an individual possession: institutions may prescribe it, absence may communicate
without deliberate intention, and its aggregate effect may exceed what any one
participant intends.

#### Culture-mechanical object

A **culture-mechanical object** is any program, carrier, implementation
arrangement, processor state, or emergent collective organization whose relevant
function is to install, activate, transmit, execute, synchronize, or enforce
culturally learned social and evaluative classifications. The term is proposed by
the present framework as a broad analytical umbrella. It does not make every
member of the category the same kind of thing.

| Culture-mechanical object | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Program complex** | A large, internally structured package capable of programming an operative mythological lens | Religions, Marxism, National Socialism, liberal constitutional culture, nationalism, company cultures, activist movements, military ethos, professional cultures, state programmes |
| **Program unit or content** | Material from which a larger operative weave is constructed | Mythologies, narratives, causal stories, moral rules, roles, exemplars, sacred values, master signifiers |
| **Carrier or activation device** | A token through which the program is communicated, activated, or made publicly legible | Flags, veils, blue neckerchiefs, imposed yellow stars, badges, certificates, slogans, linguistic forms, masks, salutes, songs, whistle signals |
| **Implementation arrangement** | A durable structure that rewards, sanctions, repeats, or institutionalizes the program | Schools, appointment systems, rituals, calendars, dress codes, promotion criteria, laws, media routines, organizational systems |
| **Local execution** | The installed program as executed by an individual sentiment classifier | A local bigOther applying its mythological lens |
| **Collective formation** | The emergent result of compatible local execution and reciprocal signalling | Mechanical solidarity and the distributed bigOther |
| **Collective agent** | A sufficiently integrated and continuous collective formation capable of judgment, intention, ownership, and action | An operative multi-mind-self |

A culturally legible phrase, song, image, hero, or ritual that evokes a shared
sublimation remains a **carrier or activation device**, an **ideological symbol**,
a master-signifier-like token, or a fragment of mythology according to its actual
function. The Wegner-facing expression **sublimation emblem** may be useful as
explanatory shorthand for its compressed representative role, but it does not
create a new culture-mechanical object type. Analysis must keep separate the
sublimation process, its resulting character or practice, the cultural
sublimation architecture that recurrently produces it, and the token that
activates relevant programming.

In common contemporary political, organizational, and media usage,
**narrative** is often a fuzzy synonym for **mythology**. Culture mechanics retains
a precise boundary inside that overlap. *Narrative* is the broader term for an
organized account of events, actors, causes, and consequences. A narrative becomes
culture-mechanically mythological when it also carries socially learned
evaluation, roles, causal interpretation, expected feeling, or permission--when it
teaches not only what happened but what should feel admirable, shameful,
legitimate, treacherous, threatening, redemptive, permitted, or forbidden. A
merely chronological account need not be a mythology. A **causal story** is a
narrative specialized around explaining why something happened and assigning
agency or responsibility. The common political expression **“the narrative”** may
refer more broadly to an authorized mythology, mythology family, or even the lens
through which events are required to be interpreted; its precise level must be
identified from context.

At the applied level, **narrative design** and **narrative construction** are
therefore close practitioner-language synonyms for **mythology construction**.
[*Mythology Construction*](0040-mythology-construction.semantic-definition.md)
develops this relationship and identifies existing narrative-design toolkits as
partial practical implementations of the same intervention domain.

A whole religion, political formation, company culture, activist movement, or
state programme may be a **composite culture-mechanical object** containing several
of these subtypes. The name *Christianity*, *Islam*, *Judaism*, *Marxism*,
*National Socialism*, or *company culture* may refer in one sentence to a body of
programming and in another to its institutions, carriers, synchronized population,
or collective agency. Analysis must therefore identify which level is meant rather
than treating the inherited name as a precise ontology.

Within that wider category, a large internally structured cultural program whose
packages are mutually fitted is a **Culture Program Complex**, shortened where
unambiguous to **program complex**. The composite culture-mechanical object remains
broader because it may join programs, carriers, institutions, fields, outputs, and
processes across analytical levels. The canonical program terminology, its
reality-answerability branches, and its ecology are defined in
[*Culture Program Ecology*](0060-culture-program-ecology.semantic-definition.md).

#### Disagreement, mythological rivalry, and ideological-form conflict

The broad adjective *ideological* must not turn every disagreement into one
undifferentiated “ideological conflict.” Culture mechanics distinguishes the
object and intensity of conflict:

| Term | Culture-mechanical meaning |
|---|---|
| **Propositional disagreement** | Incompatible truth claims that need not involve rival governing lenses. |
| **Causal-story conflict** | Incompatible accounts of agents, causes, intentions, responsibility, or remedy. |
| **Classification conflict** | Incompatible assignments of identity, status, purity, legitimacy, or permission. |
| **Mythological rivalry** | Informal or decentralized competition among meaning systems, including lifestyles, religions, political outlooks, and institutional cultures. |
| **Mythological conflict** | Active interference with a rival mythology's transmission, authority, membership, or institutional embodiment. |
| **Cultural-program conflict** | Competition between incompatible governing programs or operative lenses, especially where single-slot replacement is at stake. |
| **Ideological-form conflict** | Organized recruitment, formation, conformity testing, delegitimation, and interference with a rival program's reproduction. |
| **Fundamentalist-form conflict** | Ideological-form conflict in which expansion, retention, punishment, expulsion, or elimination authorizes or uses physical coercion or violence. |

> **Rivalry becomes conflict when participants begin actively interfering with the
> rival mythology's transmission, authority, membership, or institutional
> embodiment.**

Mythological rivalry can therefore persist without organized conflict. The
transition can occur through recruitment campaigns, polemical unbraiding,
conformity tests, delegitimation, exclusion from channels, institutional
displacement, or censorship. [*Mythology
Construction*](0040-mythology-construction.semantic-definition.md), “Mythological
replacement: braiding, apologetics, and polemics,” develops these operations and
the problem of installing a successor weave.

Kenneth Minogue calls the organized struggle **ideological conflict**. In the
preface to the second edition, dated January 2006 (p. xvi), of [*Alien Powers: The Pure Theory of
Ideology*](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781351321556_A31471797/preview-9781351321556_A31471797.pdf),
he argues that an initial move is to disable opposition, ideally by censorship.
In Culture Mechanics terminology, this maps most directly to
**ideological-form conflict**, and to **fundamentalist-form conflict** when
physical coercion or violence becomes the means of expansion or retention.
Minogue's narrower, mobilizing, conflict-centered conception must not be silently
equated with the broad religion-inclusive adjective *ideological* used in this
document.

Call censorship or channel exclusion **rival-signal suppression** when it obstructs
a competing mythology's signals, and **counter-program suppression** when it
attacks the rival program's reproduction or institutional embodiment. A
counter-narrative is not analytically random noise: it may carry a rival mythology,
independent evidence, an internal correction, minority perception, or innovation.
For the relationship between this distinction and the physical noise-suppression
heuristic, see [*Analogical Models in Culture Mechanics: A Semantic
Inventory*](0050-culture-mechanics-analogies.semantic-inventory.md), “Positive
feedback, noise, and rival signals.” These conflict terms are project-developed
translations; they are not Minogue's taxonomy.

#### Ideological symbols must be classified by function

> **An ideological symbol is a material object, word, linguistic form, gesture,
> sound, image, ritual, omission, or other socially legible token that activates
> an installed ideological program and communicates a person's relation to that
> program.**

The medium is incidental to the definition. A piece of cloth, grammatical ending,
salute, profile image, whistle pattern, silence, refusal to applaud, or continued
association with an excluded person can perform the same operation when
participants know how to decode it. A symbol can be simultaneously functional in
another domain: a mask can be a medical device and also acquire a moral-political
meaning; clothing can protect from weather and also display membership. The
presence of one function neither proves nor excludes the other.

Ideological symbols can perform several distinguishable functions:

1. activate an installed program;
2. cue the carrier's conduct;
3. display affiliation or membership;
4. distinguish insiders from outsiders;
5. advertise the program or invite imitation;
6. test conformity and expose deviation;
7. assign or expose a sanctionable out-group;
8. synchronize attention, affect, identity, judgment, or action;
9. manufacture apparent consensus;
10. mark refusal as moral or character failure;
11. communicate a claim to wider social jurisdiction;
12. enable social, institutional, legal, or physical sanctions.

No one token necessarily performs every function. Function must be inferred from
the surrounding program, expectations, audience, treatment of refusal, and actual
institutional consequences rather than from material form, religious or secular
origin, or the stated intention of one carrier.

#### Conformity signals

> **An ideological symbol becomes a conformity signal when its performance,
> display, absence, refusal, or substitution makes conformity and deviation
> publicly legible and thereby enables monitoring, reinforcement, sanction,
> recruitment, exclusion, or a claim to wider jurisdiction.**

A **boundary signal** distinguishes insiders from outsiders. A **propagation
signal** is designed or selected for easy public reproduction and advertises,
normalizes, or spreads the program. A secret greeting may mainly mark a boundary;
a slogan, salute, hashtag, linguistic convention, pledge, or clothing expectation
may invite or pressure everyone to repeat it. One symbol can perform both
functions.

#### Targeting signals

Conformity signals and target markers are not mutually exclusive material classes.
They name functions that may overlap in the same token or expectation:

> **A targeting signal is any performed, omitted, imposed, or attributed socially
> legible token that assigns a person or population to a sanctionable out-group and
> thereby makes exclusion, coercion, persecution, or violence easier to direct.**

Here **sanctionable** means treated by the program as available for sanction; it
does not mean that the classification or sanction is justified.

> **A conformity signal becomes a targeting signal when its absence, refusal,
> substitution, or incorrect performance makes the nonconforming person legible as
> a sanctionable outsider.**

The relevant **signal polarity** is how the classification becomes visible, not
whether the carrier voluntarily communicates it:

| Mode | Culture-mechanical operation | Vivid example |
|---|---|---|
| **Performed conformity** | Display marks the person as one of “us” | Blue neckerchief, expected mask, badge, salute, pledge, or prescribed linguistic form |
| **Negative targeting through nonperformance** | Absence, refusal, substitution, or incorrect performance identifies the deviant | Missing neckerchief, unmasked face where masking is expected, absent certificate, refused salute, or unapproved wording |
| **Imposed target marking** | A hostile authority forces the out-group classification onto its target | The yellow star imposed on Jews under National Socialism |
| **Attributed target marking** | A name, accusation, record, database entry, or administrative category assigns target status without requiring a visible token | “Heretic,” “class enemy,” “extremist,” “unvaccinated,” or a corresponding institutional record when the classification routes sanctions |

The blue neckerchief and the yellow star therefore differ in their direction of
assignment but can converge downstream. The neckerchief marks the compliant member
and its absence can expose a nonconforming target. The yellow star directly and
forcibly marks the person whom the governing program classifies as an outsider.
Both make a culturally defined boundary operational by making persons sortable.

COVID-19 supplies a particularly clear mixed case. An unmasked face could become a
negative targeting signal wherever masking was expected and refusal was treated as
evidence of irresponsibility, danger, or defective character. Vaccination status
was normally not visible by itself; certificates, database checks, disclosure
requirements, denied access, and verbal classification made it legible. The
official phrase [“pandemic of the
unvaccinated”](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Vaccination-Requirements-Report.pdf)
turned a biomedical status into a named causal category within public discourse
and was explicitly joined to vaccination requirements. In a given setting, whether
this classification became a targeting signal must be tested by examining blame,
expected displays, access conditions, sanctions, and the treatment of refusal.
This functional analysis does not by itself decide the empirical accuracy,
medical value, legality, proportionality, or moral legitimacy of a mask or vaccine
policy.

The decisive operation can therefore be negative. Where a display is expected, its
absence identifies the person who has not complied without requiring that person
to make a declaration. The signal thereby turns conduct into public telemetry:

```text
performance or visible omission
              ↓
conformity and deviation become legible
              ↓
peers and institutions classify and respond
              ↓
visible conformity produces apparent consensus
              ↓
apparent consensus raises the expected cost of refusal
              ↓
further public conformity
              ↺
```

When the performance is mutually observable, it can also generate **common
knowledge**: participants know the signal, know that others received it, and know
that others can observe their own response. [Michael Suk-Young Chwe's *Rational
Ritual*](https://doi.org/10.23943/princeton/9780691158280.001.0001) supplies this
coordination mechanism. Common knowledge can organize outward conduct without
proving private assent or the complete culture-mechanical account.

This loop can produce sincere convergence, strategic compliance, preference
falsification, pluralistic ignorance, or a mixture of them. [Timur Kuran's
*Private Truths, Public
Lies*](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3527602W/Private_Truths_Public_Lies)
supplies the established neighbouring mechanism in which public expression diverges
from private preference under pressure. [Václav Havel's *The Power of the
Powerless*](https://havel.fiu.edu/about-us/publications-and-resources/the-power-of-the-powerless.pdf)
shows how ritual public conformity can reproduce a system without requiring
private belief. Culture mechanics adds the local-processor, symbolic-protocol, and
field account; neither author proposed this complete synthesis.

The installed program also supplies a virtual **yardstick of the good member**: a
good hunter, vegan, worker, believer, citizen, revolutionary, or comrade. The
conformity signal makes a person's apparent position against that yardstick
visible. Assenting to an absurd statement can therefore become an especially
powerful loyalty test: agreement with “2 + 2 = 5” communicates little about
arithmetic but much about willingness to submit. Signals can also mark enemies.
Public rejection of a stigmatized word, object, person, or association may become
its own proof of belonging.

A compulsory conformity signal is not automatically a **credibility-enhancing
display** or CRED. In Joseph Henrich's narrower model, costly conduct by a model can
make professed commitment credible; see [“The Evolution of Costly Displays,
Cooperation and Religion”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2009.03.005).
With a compulsory symbol, the principal cost may fall on refusal rather than on
performance, and compliance may be purely strategic. **Sanction-backed conformity
signal**, **compulsory membership signal**, or **public loyalty marker** is then
more precise. No unvalidated checklist or numerical threshold should be presented
as a CRED measure.

#### *Beseelung* and four propagation and enforcement forms

The originating **dead--alive** analogy distinguishes information that is merely
known from programming that animates conduct. A relatively dead rule resembles
“stop at the red pedestrian light”: a person can notice and ignore it without much
guilt. An alive practice can make omission feel like personal failure, guilt, or
betrayal. The German ***beseelt***--literally, in a poetic translation,
“ensouled”--better conveys this experienced animation than the bloodless word
*stored*.

**Beseelung is an independent axis.** It concerns how affectively alive and
conduct-directing a program is, not how widely it recruits or how aggressively it
enforces. A private dietary or yoga practice can be intensely alive. A football
crowd can synchronize powerfully without seeking converts. A perfectly conforming
functionary can privately believe nothing, and a fundamentalist system can be
enforced by cynical officials.

When a programme contains an agent-model, ***agentified Beseelung*** can make that
agent sufficiently present within local evaluation that the person begins to
perceive, judge, and act from its represented perspective. This joins the
dead--alive axis to bigOther-presence without making them synonyms: a practice can
be deeply *beseelt* without a personified Thou, and an asserted vision or voice
does not by itself establish durable affective animation, distributed agency, or
fusion.

Relative deadness can also be a historical remainder. A culture may preserve a
rule, word, symbol, institution, or habitual procedure after losing the mythology,
rituals, practices, and transmission relations that once made it sacred,
identity-bearing, and synchronizing. Such a remnant can remain functionally useful:
the pedestrian signal still coordinates movement even when it no longer evokes
guilt, sacrifice, or an experience of collective being. A later observer who sees
only that remnant cannot safely infer that the rule was always affectively dead.

This creates a three-way calm-period ambiguity: an intact cultural system may be
**dormant**; surviving material may be **de-animated**; or the former
synchronization technology may be **culturally lost** and require successor
construction rather than reactivation. Loss of that technology does not empty the
one PCI slot: another operative mythological weave still occupies the local
bigOther. [*Epistemic Agency under Mechanical Solidarity*, “Observability,
cultural forgetting, and contemporary
witnesses”](0020-epistemic-agency.semantic-definition.md#observability-cultural-forgetting-and-contemporary-witnesses)
states the historical hypothesis and evidential problem. [*Analogical Models in
Culture Mechanics: A Semantic
Inventory*](0050-culture-mechanics-analogies.semantic-inventory.md) maps them to
the nested quantum, laser, magnetism, weak-field gravity, and contemporary-witness
pictures.

The private--collective--ideological--fundamentalist distinction classifies
dominant **propagation and enforcement configurations**, not different kinds of
bigOther and not a necessary sequence through which every program passes:

| Form | Characteristic propagation | Typical symbolic function | Treatment of nonparticipation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Private** | Self-selection and personal discovery | Self-cue, personal commitment, optional recognition | Normally irrelevant to outsiders; private relational boundaries may still be strong |
| **Collective** | Attraction, affiliation, and display of the pleasures of belonging | Membership, shared enjoyment, ritual synchronization, advertisement of the community | Usually tolerated, although reputation and coordinated ostracism become possible |
| **Ideological form** | Active recruitment, education, and formation in the “correct” way of being | Public conformity, loyalty tests, mission, and apparent unanimity | Moral, social, educational, or institutional pressure |
| **Fundamentalist** | Militant expansion, retention, or defence | Mobilization, enemy marking, surveillance, and demonstrations of irreversible commitment | Authorization or willingness to use physical coercion or violence to convert, retain, punish, expel, or eliminate |

**Vivid ideal-type examples:**

- In the **private form**, consider a dietary or yoga practice found and adopted on
  one's own initiative. Communities of practice may exist, but the program does not
  strongly recruit outsiders.
- In the **collective form**, consider football-club or pop-star fandom. It attracts
  through advertising, spectacle, and the visible pleasures of belonging rather
  than demanding conversion.
- In the **ideological form**, consider a proselytizing religion or a political
  movement such as Marxism. Mission, education, cadre formation, or comparable
  training forms members in the “correct” way of being.
- In the **fundamentalist form**, consider a militant religious or political
  movement willing to use bodily force to convert or retain members, punish
  refusal, expel opponents, or eliminate rivals.

These examples make the forms concrete; they do not establish that every instance
belongs wholly to one form. Whole
religions, cultures, institutions, or movements may nevertheless be classified as
predominantly one form when the scope, evidence, countercases, and possible
falsifiers are stated.

The taxonomy is a project-developed synthesis, not a classification attributed to
[Susan Blackmore's account of memes and
temes](https://www.ted.com/talks/susan_blackmore_memes_and_temes). Her replicator
account supplies a neighbouring way to think about copied cultural information;
the four forms classify the propagation and enforcement configurations in which a
program is reproduced. Their variability illustrates the originating software
analogy: programs are flexible--that is the force of *soft* in *software*.

The forms compress several axes that must remain separately inspectable:

- depth of *Beseelung*;
- public visibility;
- outward recruitment or proselytism;
- inward retention and treatment of exit;
- lateral intolerance of rival allegiance;
- jurisdiction over domains of life;
- synchronization strength;
- social and institutional sanctions;
- authorization or willingness to use physical violence.

Strong conviction is not by itself fundamentalism. Proselytism is not jealousy;
jealousy is not physical violence; and discursive violence, defined below, can
occur before the fundamentalist form.

#### Jealous programs, solidarity, and collective agents

The originating analogy is deliberately vivid:

> **A jealous multi-mind-self resembles a jealous lover who wants to own the
> beloved and cannot tolerate divided allegiance.**

The corresponding operational definition is:

> **A jealous cultural program claims exclusive allegiance, classifies
> incompatible commitment, dissent, or exit as betrayal, and mobilizes symbols,
> sanctions, institutions, and collective affect to defend that claim.**

[Kenneth Minogue's *Alien Powers: The Pure Theory of
Ideology*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Alien_Powers.html?id=XWTiQJx_u5kC)
provides a close published neighbour in its treatment of ideological revelation as
jealous and expansionary. **Jealous cultural program** and its integration with
the present ontology are project-developed terms, not Minogue's complete theory.

Jealousy must be attributed at the correct level:

| Level | Operation in a jealous configuration |
|---|---|
| **Lacan's big Other** | Carries publicly intelligible categories and tokens such as loyalty, betrayal, idolatry, heresy, oath, flag, scarf, and rival symbol. It is the protocol, not the jealous agent. |
| **Local bigOther** | Executes a jealous lens and makes rival allegiance, refusal, or exit feel shameful, contaminating, frightening, or treacherous. In **bigOther-presence**, and especially **bigOther-as-Thou**, the person may directly feel a present jealous Other that claims fidelity and refuses divided allegiance. Calling the processor itself jealous remains functional shorthand for the program it executes; calling the experienced presence jealous describes how that operation can be lived. |
| **Mechanical solidarity** | Synchronizes compatible classifications and reciprocal enforcement. |
| **Distributed bigOther** | Preserves the network state through expectations, reputations, rituals, institutions, symbols, and sanctions. |
| **Multi-mind-self** | If the collective-agent threshold is met, the integrated system can itself claim, condemn, exclude, punish, or attack through its members and institutions. |

Jealousy therefore has both an **analytical description** and a
**phenomenological appearance**. Analytically, the installed program defines
exclusive loyalty and the local bigOther executes its classifications.
Phenomenologically, a jealous God, Nation, People, History, or Cause may be
encountered as personally present--watching, addressing, claiming, accusing, or
demanding sacrifice. This direct individual experience does not collapse the
local processor into Lacan's shared symbolic protocol, and it does not by itself
establish that the surrounding distributed bigOther has crossed the
multi-mind-self threshold.

**Single-slot exclusivity is not jealousy.** Every stable program fully occupies
the one local slot, including an open, pluralistic, lifted program. Jealousy begins
when the installed program additionally demands exclusive allegiance, treats
legitimate rivalry or replacement as betrayal, blocks dignified exit, claims the
whole person, exceeds its jurisdiction, or monopolizes authoritative
interpretation.

Jealousy and proselytism are hypothesized to reinforce one another often,
especially in ideological-form and fundamentalist programs, but they are not
identical. A separatist sect can retain members jealously while seeking no
converts; a missionary movement can recruit energetically while respecting
refusal and exit. Analysis should therefore record outward recruitment, inward
retention, rival intolerance, jurisdiction, and enforcement separately.

[Exodus 20:3 and
20:5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2020%3A3-5&version=NRSVUE)
supply the theological exemplar of a jealous God, exclusive covenant loyalty, and
“no other gods.” The jealous-lover comparison uses this as an analogy for the
classification of rival attachment as betrayal or idolatry, not as empirical proof
of the social mechanism. The related Christian interpretation--that the human
capacity to join a multi-mind-self is the “God-shaped hole” that should be filled
by God rather than a human ideological collective--is an originating confessional
interpretation, not a general scientific proposition or a settled philological
claim.

#### Moralization as character accusation: Andrick's bridge

[Michael Andrick's *Im Moralgefängnis: Spaltung verstehen und
überwinden*](https://derandrick.de/) supplies the missing micro-mechanism between
moral classification, conformity signalling, exclusion, and escalating violence.
In Andrick's account, misplaced moralization performs two changes at once:

1. **Focus:** the exchange moves from the disputed issue to the other person's
   character.
2. **Mode:** inquiry and argument become accusation and prosecution.

His kindergarten example preserves the operation vividly. A discussion about
whether a kindergarten should have two or three floors becomes a trial of whether
one participant is a good person who deserves to remain in the discussion. A
fleeting statement or minor act is read as a revealing disclosure of character;
the alleged disclosure becomes a character accusation; and the person's standing,
rather than the proposition, becomes the target:

```text
factual disagreement or missing conformity signal
              ↓
statement, omission, or association becomes “character disclosure”
              ↓
character accusation
              ↓
inquiry becomes prosecution
              ↓
discursive standing is attacked
              ↓
submit, recant, or be excluded
```

Andrick's strong verdict is that **moralization is discursive violence** and makes
physical violence more likely. Its immediate domination does not require bodily
force: it attacks the other's recognized standing and invokes the background
threat, “You may not be breaking public law, but you are breaking my law; submit or
I will exclude you.” Even legitimate moral condemnation remains aggressive in
this account. In private relationships it is the metaphorical **atomic bomb**, or
the sharpest social weapon, although consciously removing an intolerable person
from one's voluntary private circle may itself be legitimate.

The tendency toward violence therefore cannot simply be abolished in Andrick's
account: even justified moral condemnation contains an exclusionary threat. His
associated freedom claim is equally strong--pathological moralization and free
self-determination cannot coexist. Official voluntariness does not settle the
question when refusing a prescribed linguistic form, dress expectation, ritual,
or declaration brings foreseeable moral, social, or professional punishment.

Culture mechanics extends rather than merely repeats Andrick's account:

```text
local bigOther classifies a person as bad, disloyal, or unbearable
              ↓ encoded through
shared accusation, stigma, required denunciation, or conformity test
              ↓ reciprocally reinforced as
mechanical solidarity and collective exclusion
              ↓ preserved through
distributed reputations, institutional routines, records, and sanctions
              ↓ possibly executed by
a multi-mind-self capable of organized judgment and action
```

**Guilt by association is a secondary conformity signal.** Continuing to speak
with an excluded person, refusing to denounce them, or preserving a public
relationship can itself be treated as a character disclosure. Cancel culture,
refusal to enter discussion with opponents, mandatory denunciation, and the public
sorting of people into epistemic tribes are candidate manifestations of this
mechanism; each case still requires evidence that the operation actually occurred.

Division is therefore not merely a static condition. It can be continuously
enacted as people stop speaking with opponents, speak only about them, infer
character from statements, demand public denunciation, and punish continued
association. The present framework adds the **moral-closure hypothesis**:

Rudolf Bilz's formulation of human existence as **tribunal existence** and [Odo
Marquard's “tribunalization of modern lived
reality”](https://www.wiko-berlin.de/fileadmin/Jahrbuchberichte/1982/1982_83_Marquard_Odo_Jahrbuchbericht.pdf)
are neighbouring diagnoses of a condition in which persons are placed under
pervasive accusation and pressure to justify their right to be as they are. The
exact primary source for Bilz's formulation remains to be verified; it is retained
here as a provenance lead rather than presented as established evidence for the
mechanism.

```text
group bundles around an argument
              ↓
non-bundlers are excluded
              ↓
belonging becomes membership among “good people”
              ↓
opposition becomes character defect
              ↓
an open society is converted into a closed community
              ↓
the closed community seeks dominance
```

The originating discussion connected this sequence with Ulrike Guérot's
“bundling” description of a group organizing around an argument and excluding
those who refuse to bundle, and with Ferdinand Tönnies's distinction between open
societal association and closed community. That compound attribution still
requires a primary-source audit. The diagram above is therefore Culture
Mechanics' moral-closure hypothesis, not a definition attributed to Guérot,
Tönnies, or an established general definition of fascism. Moral closure is one
possible pathway into, but is not sufficient for, the conjunctive
[whole-polity definition of Fascism](0020-epistemic-agency.semantic-definition.md#fascism-as-a-strong-whole-polity-realization).
When closure becomes a concrete attempt to align multiple independent societal
domains toward one privileged ideological direction, test the separate
[polity-scale *Gleichschaltung*
hazard](0060-culture-program-ecology.semantic-definition.md#polity-scale-gleichschaltung-hazard).
Do so even before a complete Culture Program can be reconstructed or attributed.
Moral closure alone does not establish that process gate.

The originating **ideology-as-hallucination hypothesis** is stronger still: an
ideology operating as a collectively protected hallucination cannot win through a
reality-facing explanation of mechanisms and consequences and therefore tends to
use discursive domination. On this hypothesis, moralization expresses fear--or,
in the culpable case, knowledge--that the position would fail under fair
conditions among equals. This is a definite and risky project hypothesis, not an
established result of Andrick's book. It would be weakened by cases in which a
position systematically moralizes strong opponents despite repeatedly prevailing
under open, symmetrical, evidence-sensitive contest, or by evidence that the
moralization is better explained without awareness or fear of argumentative
weakness.

#### The enforcement--exclusion and violence repertoire

The originating claim is:

> **Enforcement and exclusion are dual operations. Enforcement states or applies
> a condition of continued inclusion, standing, access, or permitted action;
> exclusion withdraws or denies these goods when the condition is not met.**

Andrick's compact threat--“Submit, or I will exclude you”--contains both sides.
From the program's perspective, the rule is enforced. From the affected person's
perspective, refusal can mean exclusion from moral standing, discussion,
relationship, community, institution, territory, or life. The same sanction often
does both simultaneously: excluding one visible nonconformist also enforces
conformity among everyone who observes the consequence.

This is a structural duality, not an assertion that enforcement and exclusion are
identical in every case. Instruction, persuasion, reward, or ordinary coordination
can maintain a practice without applying an exclusionary sanction. A person can
end a private relationship without trying to govern a wider public. Physical
coercion can be used to retain or convert a person rather than expel them, although
it excludes their alternative conduct and violates bodily autonomy. Jurisdiction,
proportionality, evidence, due process, and the good being withheld remain separate
questions.

The word *violence* must likewise not hide the difference between moral
evaluation, discursive domination, exclusion, coercion, and bodily force:

| Form | Program-side enforcement | Excluded standing or domain |
|---|---|---|
| **Moral evaluation** | Classifies conduct as good or bad; ordinary bigOther activity, not necessarily moralization | Moral approval or recognition as a good member; no civic or social exclusion need follow |
| **Discursive violence** | Replaces inquiry with character accusation and attacks the opponent's standing | Credibility, admissibility, or recognition as a legitimate interlocutor |
| **Relational exclusion** | Conditions or ends friendship, intimacy, or voluntary private association | A particular relationship or private circle |
| **Collective social exclusion** | Uses ostracism, cancellation, guilt by association, or coordinated reputational destruction | Community membership, social contact, reputation, and informal opportunities |
| **Institutional coercion** | Conditions employment, education, platforms, services, office, or professional standing on submission | Access to institutions, occupations, services, platforms, or public roles |
| **Physical violence** | Uses or threatens bodily force to compel, retain, punish, convert, expel, or eliminate | Bodily autonomy, movement, safety, territory, or the permitted alternative course of action |
| **Eliminative violence** | Treats the rival as having no right to remain, participate, or exist | Membership, territory, public existence, life, or memory |

The conformity-signal mechanism is the sensor and this repertoire supplies possible
outputs:

```text
expected performance, imposed mark, or attributed label
                         ↓
membership, conformity, and deviation become legible
                  /                         \
       inclusion and reinforcement     exclusion and punishment
                  \                         /
             future conformity becomes more likely
```

This repertoire is not a deterministic ladder. Moralization can stop at private
withdrawal or social exclusion, and movements can jump to institutional or
physical coercion. The compact distinction is:

> **All moralization is discursively violent in Andrick's sense; not all
> moralization expresses willingness to use physical violence. The fundamentalist
> threshold is authorization or willingness to use physical or eliminative force,
> not the mere presence of moral aggression.**

Moralization can nevertheless raise the probability of further violence by
turning the mistaken person into the bad person, licensing exclusion, making
punishment appear deserved, and allowing aggression to be experienced as
protection, justice, purification, or duty. This is a probabilistic escalation
hypothesis, not the claim that every moralized disagreement becomes physically
violent.

#### Stanton's genocidal escalation processes as a neighbouring model

[Gregory H. Stanton's *Ten Stages of
Genocide*](https://www.genocidewatch.com/tenstages) supplies a neighbouring model
of how collective classification and symbolization can become organized
eliminative violence. Stanton now emphasizes that the “stages” are overlapping,
non-linear processes: they can operate simultaneously, need not advance
inexorably, and can be interrupted. His object is the specific process leading to
genocide; the culture-mechanical repertoire is broader and also applies to
non-genocidal programs, relationships, institutions, and conflicts.

The models therefore map onto one another without becoming synonyms:

| Stanton's process | Culture-mechanical relationship |
|---|---|
| **1. Classification** | The mythological lens divides a population into “us” and “them.” Classification is upstream of violence and can also serve benign or necessary distinctions. |
| **2. Symbolization** | Names, dress, documents, and other tokens make the classification socially legible. An expected conformity signal can target through its absence; an imposed marker such as the yellow star directly marks the out-group; an attributed label can do so without a worn object. |
| **3. Discrimination** | Enforcement--exclusion becomes durable law, custom, or institutional practice that removes rights, standing, access, or opportunity from the classified group. |
| **4. Dehumanization** | Moralized character accusation expands into categorical enemy construction: persons become vermin, disease, contamination, pollution, or another kind of being whose removal can be redescribed as cleansing or protection. |
| **5. Organization** | Parties, bureaucracies, police, armies, militias, media systems, lists, and surveillance turn hostility into scalable implementation capacity. Organization is infrastructure for violence, not by itself another form of violence. |
| **6. Polarization** | Moral closure, guilt by association, communicative severance, and attacks on moderates disable countercultures, compromise, and independent correction. |
| **7. Preparation** | Plans, target lists, training, weapons, mobilization, and euphemistic goals make organized physical or eliminative intent operational. These are strong indicators that the fundamentalist threshold has been crossed, although no numbered Stanton process defines that threshold. |
| **8. Persecution** | Institutional coercion and physical violence become coordinated identification, dispossession, arrest, displacement, confinement, torture, or killing of the target population. |
| **9. Extermination** | Genocide is a specific collective form of eliminative violence. The culture-mechanical category is broader because programs can seek to eliminate political, social, or other rivals without satisfying the definition of genocide. |
| **10. Denial** | The program protects itself after and during violence through euphemism, destroyed evidence, impunity, blame reversal, suppression of memory, and attacks on witnesses. This connects the enforcement--exclusion and violence repertoire to [memory control and systemic lying in *Epistemic Constitution of Lifted Mechanical Solidarity*](0030-epistemic-constitution.semantic-definition.md#14-memory-provenance-and-correction). |

Stanton's model supplies historically developed observations and an early-warning
process model. It does not establish the local-bigOther processor, distributed
field, jealousy, or multi-mind-self ontology proposed here. Conversely, Culture
Mechanics proposes a possible micro-to-macro mechanism through which some of
Stanton's processes become affectively compelling, mutually observable, and
institutionally executable.

The connection is a risky escalation hypothesis, not guilt by conceptual
association. A classification, symbol, health rule, access condition, or act of
private exclusion does not establish a genocidal trajectory. The inference becomes
stronger only as additional processes appear: a sanctionable out-group,
dehumanizing mythology, destruction of moderating relations, scalable coercive
organization, preparation for physical force, persecution, and elimination. The
absence of these transitions, durable protection of the target group's equal civic
standing, and effective correction of wrongful classifications count against the
escalation hypothesis.

#### Private morality and public standing

The originating maxim **“morality is private”** protects an essential boundary:
private moral revulsion does not by itself decide who may participate in public
life. Taken absolutely, however, it would exclude genuine public moral questions
and legitimate public authority. The compatible formulation is:

> **Comprehensive personal moral convictions do not by themselves determine
> another person's civic standing. Public coercion requires publicly contestable
> rules, proper jurisdiction, evidence, proportionality, and due process.**

Or, more vividly:

> **We may debate moral questions publicly, but disagreement must not become a
> character trial that revokes the opponent's right to participate.**

An imposed target label is not the target's self-description. Nuclear taboo
vocabulary such as “Nazi” may operate as a targeting signal or as one stage of
the projective-vandalism circuit; it does not by itself establish the attributed
Culture Program or hazard class. The
[Attribution-Source
Asymmetry](0060-culture-program-ecology.semantic-definition.md#165-attribution-source-asymmetry-and-cross-audience-audit)
governs that evidentiary distinction.

This boundary does not prohibit private association or moral judgment. It prevents
a private moral law from silently becoming public jurisdiction. Its institutional
development belongs in [*Epistemic Constitution of Lifted Mechanical

“Low-cost contradiction, not low-cost condemnation.”

#### Worked examples and boundary tests

**The DDR blue neckerchief.** The blue neckerchief of the Pioneer organization was
not merely clothing or a membership badge. In a school environment where almost
every child was expected to be a Pioneer, wearing it displayed conformity while
the missing scarf exposed refusal without requiring the child to speak. The
[historical description by
MDR](https://www.mdr.de/geschichte/ddr/alltag/erziehung-bildung/ernst-thaelmann-pioniere-halstuch-ddr-100.html)
records that Pioneer membership was part of ordinary school life and served the
state's socialist education. The culture-mechanical interpretation--boundary
marker, identity and loyalty signal, public compliance device, and trigger for
monitoring or sanction--is the present framework's synthesis.

**Speech and conduct.** Prescribed gendered language (*Gendern*) can perform the
same operation as cloth when use displays conformity, non-use becomes observable,
and non-use is interpreted as character disclosure. A COVID mask can remain a
medical device while also functioning as an ideological signal when the
surrounding field makes it a visible test of responsibility, obedience,
solidarity, distrust, or political allegiance. A salute, pledge, pronoun formula,
badge, ribbon, flag, profile image, applause, whistle, silence, or continued
association can likewise carry the operation. The test is functional and
contextual, not material.

**Kippah and Islamic veiling.** The originating whole-religion comparison is
deliberately asymmetric and testable. Judaism is hypothesized to be predominantly
non-proselytizing and the kippah predominantly member-directed: its ordinary public
meaning is the wearer's observance and affiliation, not a demand that surrounding
non-Jews adopt it. Islam, considered as a whole religious-cultural program, is
hypothesized to be predominantly proselytizing and jealous. Islamic veiling can
therefore function simultaneously as personal observance, internal conformity,
outward propagation, and a claim to public moral jurisdiction. This classification
concerns the operative canonical and institutional program and its aggregate
trajectory; it does not assert that every Jew, Muslim, kippah wearer, or veiled
woman understands or intends the complete operation.

On this hypothesis, veiling can become a **public threat** in a precise
culture-mechanical sense: not because the fabric or every individual wearer issues
a conscious threat, but because the surrounding program publicly represents a
demand for wider jurisdiction and makes sanctions against eventual refusal
predictable if that program becomes dominant. The kippah contrast predicts no
comparable Judaism-wide apparatus for punishing refusal or compelling outsiders;
evidence of such an apparatus would count against the comparison.

The blue neckerchief and Islamic veiling are thus proposed as **functional
homologues** when both embody the program in everyday life, make conformity and
refusal legible, distinguish good members from questionable ones, enable peer or
institutional monitoring, normalize the underlying order, and reproduce it through
public performance. Their differences concern propagation, jealousy, jurisdiction,
and enforcement, not a categorical division between secular and religious
symbols.

**The boundary is not Iran.** Police enforcement is the extreme implementation
layer, not the beginning of a jurisdictional claim. The originating trajectory is:

```text
practice presented as personal choice
              ↓
practice promoted as protection, dignity, or empowerment
              ↓
practice becomes a public model of proper conduct
              ↓
criticism is moralized as hostility or harm
              ↓
countercultures lose legitimate means of defence
              ↓
institutions accommodate and reproduce the norm
              ↓
nonconformity becomes socially or professionally costly
              ↓
the norm acquires legal and police enforcement
```

The language of protection can create an asymmetric field: the advancing program
expresses its demand as care, freedom, dignity, or safety while resistance is
classified as prejudice or aggression. The program remains free to extend its
norm, while countercultures are morally disarmed from defending their own order.
This is the proposed **protection-framed jurisdictional manoeuvre**. It can emerge
through many sincere participants without a central conspiracy. Only when the
collective-agent threshold is met should it be attributed as an intentional
manoeuvre of an operative multi-mind-self; below that threshold it remains an emergent
operation of a program, solidarity field, and distributed bigOther.

Iran is proposed not as the boundary at which the problem begins but as an
advanced realized form in which a religious prescription has captured state
jurisdiction. The [United Nations Independent International Fact-Finding Mission
on Iran](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/hrbodies/hrcouncil/ffmi-iran/FFM-Iran-Update-13-September-2024.pdf)
documents morality-police deployment, surveillance, arrests, prosecution, and
forced pledges connected to mandatory hijab enforcement. The originating
**end-state hypothesis** is conditional and categorical: for the relevant
aggressively proselytizing and jealous Islamic movements in Western societies,
success means defeating countercultures and acquiring sufficient institutional
power; **if they succeed, Iran is the predicted end-state form**, in which promoted
Islamic norms become compulsory public jurisdiction. Iran exhibits the predicted
completed conversion; its existence does not by itself establish that every
movement will succeed.

The religion-level hypothesis should be tested against canonical claims, mission
and jihad doctrines, historical institutional trajectories, treatment of apostasy
and criticism, freedom of rival religions, rules governing women's dress, and
conduct after political dominance is obtained. It would be weakened by durable
Muslim-majority orders possessing overwhelming cultural and political power while
intrinsically protecting across generations:

- genuine freedom not to veil;
- conversion away from Islam and public criticism of Islam;
- rival religions and nonreligious life;
- equal civic standing independent of religious conformity;
- constitutional barriers preventing Islamic morality from becoming compulsory
  public law.

Judaism's contrasting classification is equally revisable: evidence of systematic
outward conversion, expanding jurisdiction over non-Jews, or coercive enforcement
of kippah observance would weaken the predominantly non-proselytizing,
member-directed hypothesis. Whole religions may be classified. The scientific
safeguard is precise scope, evidence, countercases, and falsifiability--not
protective euphemism.

### 9. First analogy: the computer with one PCI slot

The computer analogy presents the architecture in its simplest form.

Imagine a computer containing:

- a powerful general-purpose **CPU**;
- one **PCI slot**;
- a comparatively simple sentiment-classification card installed in that slot;
- a network interface through which the card exchanges signals with compatible cards.

The CPU performs complex computation. The PCI card does something much simpler: it assigns values to the CPU's current objects and possible outputs. It marks them, in effect, as attractive or aversive, honourable or shameful, legitimate or forbidden.

The card's simplicity is deceptive. If it can alter the CPU's priorities, error signals, and reward expectations, it can redirect the much more powerful processor. A simple evaluator can cause an extremely intelligent CPU to spend enormous resources defending a premise the evaluator has made emotionally non-negotiable.

The card runs a mythological lens shaped by many mythologies and potentially
assembled from several compatible programming packages. Social signals usually do
not install a whole new lens; they call mappings already present on the card.
Because the machine has only one PCI slot, a rival card cannot simply be added
beside the first, and there is no hidden runtime controller that ranks parallel
cards. Compatible packages can extend the software on the installed card. An
incompatible governing program must be rejected, or the incumbent program must be
unbraided and a successor rebraided and installed on the one card.

This analogy makes the division of labour between the two processors easy to understand. Its limitation is that real human cognition is embodied, affective, interpretive, and less cleanly modular than a desktop computer.

### 10. Second analogy: Haidt's elephant with a wireless collar

The second analogy gives a closer picture of the proposed psychological operation.

Jonathan Haidt describes conscious reasoning as a **rider** sitting on an **elephant**. The elephant represents the large, fast, intuitive, emotional, and mostly nonconscious part of the mind. The rider represents the smaller conscious and verbal part that explains, plans, and sometimes guides the elephant, but frequently rationalizes where the elephant is already going.

Culture mechanics adds a **wireless collar** around the elephant's neck.

- The **elephant** is the person's intuitive, motivational, and largely nonconscious psychology.
- The **rider** is conscious verbal awareness and explicit reasoning.
- The **processor in the wireless collar** is the bigOther.
- The **program stored in the collar** is the mythological lens.
- The **mythologies** are the evaluated stories through which that program is trained and reinforced.
- The **wireless signals** are other people's expressions, symbols, slogans, rituals, approval, disapproval, and visible conduct.

The collar can produce social pleasure and pain: pride, shame, belonging, exclusion, sacredness, disgust, reassurance, fear, or indignation. It acts directly upon the elephant. The elephant changes its posture, attention, and direction long before the rider clearly notices the collar's intervention.

Only afterward may the rider become aware of a conclusion:

> “This is obviously disgraceful.”  
> “Someone like me cannot do that.”  
> “Any decent person must support this.”

The rider may mistake the resulting feeling for the product of independent reasoning. It may then use the main processor's verbal intelligence to justify the direction in which the elephant has already begun to move.

This analogy is closer to the psychological claim than the computer analogy because it shows that the bigOther does not normally dictate complete thoughts. It modifies the affective conditions under which thought and action occur.

For the mapped features, non-inferences, and relationship of the CPU, PCI-slot,
wireless-collar, protocol, and network pictures to the framework's physical and
craft analogies, see [*Analogical Models in Culture Mechanics: A Semantic
Inventory*](0050-culture-mechanics-analogies.semantic-inventory.md).

### 11. The wireless superpower: the individual coupling interface

The bigOther's distinctive superpower is its capacity to participate in
**distributed computing**. Every collar is both receiver and transmitter. There is
no separate class of persons possessing the only transmitters. People emit social
signals through:

- facial expression, posture, and tone;
- attention or deliberate inattention;
- laughter, applause, chanting, or silence;
- confidence, fear, disgust, or outrage;
- repetition of phrases;
- imitation and conformity;
- approval, ridicule, punishment, and protection;
- participation in or refusal of rituals.

These outputs become inputs for other collars:

        ↓

        ↓

        ↓

The originating physical analogy is that a local bigOther resembles a charged
particle: it is a source of a field and is simultaneously susceptible to the field
produced by other sources. The comparison concerns relational architecture and
back-action. It does not propose a new physical force or claim that social dynamics
obey Maxwell's equations.

When participants run sufficiently compatible mythological lenses and can observe
one another, reciprocal feedback can produce rapid alignment. A crowd may begin to
attend, feel, chant, fear, celebrate, condemn, or act as if it were one body. This

### 12. Local bigOther, symbolic protocol, and distributed field

Culture mechanics distinguishes seven levels that must not be treated as synonyms:

| Level | Meaning in culture mechanics |
|---|---|
| **Lacan's big Other** | The shared symbolic order and, in the present functional translation, the on-the-wire protocol of language, law, roles, conventions, master signifiers, and recognized symbolic authority. |
| **Local bigOther** | The individual sentiment classifier that executes one installed mythological lens and transmits and receives through the shared protocol. |
| **Mechanical solidarity** | Emergent field coherence generated as compatible local bigOthers repeatedly produce, observe, and answer common classifications and sentiments. |
| **Distributed bigOther** | The dynamically maintained symbolic-affective network state reproduced through local bigOthers, institutions, roles, practices, stories, and artifacts. |
| **Multi-mind-self presentation** | The collective represented, perceived, addressed, or incarnated as one self without yet establishing collective agency. |
| **Operative multi-mind-self / multi-mind-self proper** | The distributed system when it becomes sufficiently integrated and continuous to perceive, remember, judge, intend, own, and act as a collective agent. Not every crowd, presentation, or mechanically solidary field reaches this threshold. |
| **Fused multi-mind-self** | An operative collective agent that additionally displaces participating persons as independently legitimate authors across the relevant jurisdiction. |

The relationship is generative rather than an equation:

```text
LOCAL BIGOTHERS

                    ↓ communicate through
SHARED SYMBOLIC PROTOCOL / LACANIAN BIG OTHER
                    ↓ may generate
MECHANICAL-SOLIDARITY COHERENCE
                    ↓ maintains

                    ↓ may be represented or encountered as

                    ↓ may become sufficiently integrated as
OPERATIVE MULTI-MIND-SELF
                    ↓ may additionally absorb personal standing as
FUSED MULTI-MIND-SELF
```

This is not a necessary ladder. A presentation can remain below agency, an
operative multi-mind-self can remain differentiated, and a fusion ideology can
seek absorption without producing competent collective agency. Unqualified
*multi-mind-self* may be used as family-level explanatory shorthand, but strict
claims about intention, ownership, responsibility, or fusion require the
qualified term. [*Epistemic Agency under Mechanical Solidarity*](0020-epistemic-agency.semantic-definition.md#handoff-from-the-individual-architecture)
owns the complete register rule and threshold tests.

The distributed bigOther consists of no single person or object. It is realized
through language, expectations, public symbols, rituals, stories, institutions,
roles, laws, artifacts, and the anticipated judgments of other people. It is
implemented through individuals but, as an emergent relational organization, is
not adequately explained as a mere list of independently formed individual
psychologies.

The relation remains recursive:

```text
DISTRIBUTED BIGOTHER
mythologies, institutions, symbols, rituals, expectations
                    ↓ programs and activates
LOCAL BIGOTHERS
                    ↓ influence
ELEPHANTS AND MAIN CPUs
                    ↓ produce
PUBLIC SIGNALS AND INSTITUTIONAL PRACTICES
                    ↓ reproduce or transform
DISTRIBUTED BIGOTHER
                    ↺
```

Culture is neither wholly outside nor wholly inside the person. It is the
comparatively stable pattern maintained by this circulation between local
programming and distributed execution. First-order and **lifted mechanical
solidarity** differ in the abstraction level of their shared classifications, not
in this basic field-forming mechanism. [*Epistemic Agency under Mechanical

together with field geometry, collective ownership, gatekeepers, and back-action
upon epistemic agency.

#### Nested authorship, activation, and episodic multi-mind-selves

An operative multi-mind-self is a **network-level subject**, not a second local self or a
second PCI card installed inside each participant. Collective and personal
authorship can therefore coexist at different levels. The collective can
legitimately perceive, remember, decide, own, and act as *we* while participating
persons retain legitimate *I*s, differentiated functions, consciences, judgments,
relationships, property, and bounded rights of refusal and exit.

> **A differentiated multi-mind-self adds an operative *we* without invalidating
> the participating *I*s. Fusion begins when the *we* displaces those *I*s as
> independently legitimate authors.**

The definition is functional and emergent. It does not reduce the collective to a
mere metaphor or list of individual states: the organized relation has continuing
capacities and acts back upon its participants. It also does not by itself assert
one unitary field of phenomenal consciousness analogous to a biological person's
experience. Whether collective phenomenal subjectivity exists is a further and
much harder claim than whether the distributed organization qualifies as an
agent.

The relevant integration can have different temporal profiles. A continuing
multi-mind-self may operate mainly through differentiated clockwork coordination
and enter intermittent high-gain laser episodes. An **episodic multi-mind-self**
crosses the collective-agent threshold only during bounded periods of sufficient
common attention, coupling, reciprocal feedback, memory access, judgment, and
coordinated action. Between episodes, institutions, calendars, roles, artifacts,
places, stories, and trained local bigOthers may preserve a **latent distributed
bigOther** below the presently active multi-mind-self threshold.

Calling this latency “sleep” is useful only when viable reactivation machinery and
identity-bearing continuity remain. A de-animated remnant or culturally lost
synchronization technology is not merely a sleeping collective self. The detailed
state model and its pilgrimage, military, and Subak boundary cases belong to
[*Epistemic Agency under Mechanical Solidarity*, “Activation profiles and episodic
multi-mind-selves”](0020-epistemic-agency.semantic-definition.md#activation-profiles-and-episodic-multi-mind-selves).

#### Differentiated unity and subjecthood absorption (fusion)

The existence or strength of a collective agent does not decide what standing its
members retain. Culture mechanics calls a real shared *we* **differentiated
unity** when distinct persons, functions, judgments, relationships, and legitimate
subordinate jurisdictions remain constitutive parts of the common order. The
qualifier **person-preserving unity** emphasizes the same boundary: unity is not
mere coexistence, but neither does membership dissolve the participating subjects.

**Subjecthood absorption (fusion)** is the independent extent to which a
collective program treats its members as organs whose personal judgment,
conscience, purpose, property, loyalties, identity, relationships, refusal, or
exit have no legitimate standing against the collectively enacted subject.
Equivalently, fusion is the displacement or absorption of the person's self as an
independently legitimate author, so that another enacted self increasingly
perceives, judges, speaks, owns, and acts through that person. The person's
biological body, main processor, skills, memory, and practical intelligence need
not disappear; they can remain active as the collective subject's execution
surface. The eye still sees, the mouth still speaks, and the hand still acts, but
the collective claims the standing to determine for whom and for what they act.

Subjecthood absorption is not identical to high cultural interoperability, strong
synchronization, multi-mind-self agency, comprehensive local slot occupancy, or a
large jurisdiction. Those properties can contribute to it, but none defines it.

The principal dynamics increasing absorption are called **fusion pressure**:

- one selected alignment is generalized across more domains and maintained beyond
  the task or episode that made it useful;
- independent judgment and differentiated contribution are reclassified as noise,
  selfishness, alienation, impurity, or betrayal;
- personal, household, professional, associational, and institutional
  jurisdictions are subordinated to one collective purpose;
- the collective's classifications become compulsory while correction, refusal,
  exit, and appeal lose legitimate standing.

**Total fusion** is the limiting case: high subjecthood absorption combines with
comprehensive jurisdiction and closed correction or exit. The originating image
is that persons become “drops of water in one clear pond.” It is a pole, not the
definition of every multi-mind-self presentation or operative multi-mind-self and not a biological state that humans can
literally enter.

A **fusion ideology** is a mythology or program that treats the separate person as
an incomplete, alienated, or falsely conscious fragment and seeks to reconstitute
persons as organs of one collectively enacted subject with increasingly
comprehensive jurisdiction. Such a program can demand fusion without succeeding
in forming a competent multi-mind-self; conversely, a capable collective agent can
remain differentiated, person-preserving, jurisdictionally bounded, and
corrigible.

One route into fusion is
[**Humanity inversion**](0040-mythology-construction.semantic-definition.md#humanity-as-standing-horizon-and-sacred-object):
an abstraction meant to protect the standing of concrete persons is agentified,
assigned a hidden true interest, and made into the authority under which those
persons may be consumed. Invoking Humanity alone does not establish this
pathology; the diagnosis requires the conversion from equal standing to
collective subject, privileged interpreter, and sacrificial permission.

These distinctions add a **subjecthood-absorption axis** to analysis without
altering the single-slot principle. Every participant still has one fully occupied
local slot. The question is what standing the installed weave grants the person
within the collective relation, not how many evaluators occupy that slot.

The proposed [**Lifted-Order Capacity
Dashboard**](0060-culture-program-ecology.semantic-definition.md#13101-the-lifted-order-capacity-dashboard)
operationalizes part of this person-preservation question through *Speak, Own,
Refuse, Exit,* and *Appeal*, then separately tests constitutional correction,
cooperation, material capability, and real alternatives. Its Secure Homestead
Rate treats effective owner-occupied property as one possible material anchor for
independent standing. These are candidate measurements, not additions to the
definition of subjecthood absorption.

The proposed [***Schutzbalken* principle**](0060-culture-program-ecology.semantic-definition.md#1321-democracy-as-schutzbalken-gegen-die-macht)
places this boundary inside the pluralistic classical-liberal Culture Strategy:
public and private concentrations of power must not become unbounded,
unanswerable, self-certifying, or practically inescapable jurisdictions over the
person. This is a proposed constitutional response to fusion pressure, not part
of the definition of fusion. Power, asymmetry, wealth, expertise, employment, or
collective authority do not by themselves establish subjecthood absorption; the
diagnosis still requires evidence that personal judgment, purpose, property,
refusal, exit, appeal, or other standing has been displaced.

### 13. Symbolic reality

The bigOther does not merely evaluate an independently completed physical world. It also participates in creating **social reality**.

A piece of cloth becomes a flag. A person becomes a judge, citizen, traitor, hero, or martyr. An utterance becomes a promise, verdict, oath, insult, or declaration of war. A place becomes sacred. A relationship becomes a marriage. An individual becomes the bearer of rights and dignity.

These properties are not inherent physical properties, but neither are they merely private fantasies. They are real insofar as people and institutions collectively recognize and act upon them. Symbols, rituals, and authorized classifications therefore do not only represent society; their execution helps constitute society.

Culture mechanics calls attention to both directions:

1. society programs the local bigOther; and
2. locally classified action reproduces or transforms society.

### 15. Open and closed cultural programming

Culture mechanics is descriptive before it is normative. The same mechanisms can support an open society or a closed total community.

A **closed cultural program** forms a mythological lens that teaches the bigOther to reward:

- unanimity and obedience;
- one overriding identity;
- submission to a central authority;
- denunciation of deviants;
- conflation of factual truth with political loyalty;
- exclusion of competing institutions and sources;
- treatment of counterevidence as betrayal or pollution.

An **open cultural program** forms a mythological lens that teaches the bigOther to value second-order norms:

- every person possesses dignity;
- opponents remain legitimate members of society;
- disagreement is not automatically betrayal;
- respect for a person does not require agreement with every claim;
- evidence may correct belief;
- authority may be criticized;
- compromise can be honourable;
- multiple institutions and affiliations must remain independent;
- no person or institution may become the sole authorized voice of the bigOther.

On the single-slot model, pluralism does not mean having no shared programming or
installing one independent evaluator for each affiliation. It means that one open
weave can be a fully installed lifted meta-program whose shared generator admits
different context-sensitive first-order outputs. Its concrete surface can vary
without its authority being weaker or local: the binding sameness lies in the
rules by which answers are generated, tested, applied, and revised. These rules are
not a higher runtime judge ranking several bigOthers; generator and outputs form
one weave. Detailed cases and boundary tests are developed in [*Epistemic Agency
under Mechanical Solidarity*](0020-epistemic-agency.semantic-definition.md#25-examples-cases-and-boundary-tests).
Pluralism as a reality-answerable Culture Strategy, and the distinction between
Culture Strategy and Pure-Ideology Culture Program, are developed in
[*Culture Program Ecology*](0060-culture-program-ecology.semantic-definition.md).

### 16. Autonomy, capture, and susceptibility

Culture mechanics does not define autonomy as the absence of a bigOther. Language,
morality, identity, and social cooperation all require cultural formation. Nor is
autonomy the installation of a second bigOther to overrule the first. The relevant
question is whether the main processor can inspect and revise the one installed
lens and the bigOther's classifications. Autonomy is therefore compatible with
full capture: it names an operative weave whose binding rules protect the main
processor's capacity to inspect and revise its classifications, not incomplete
programming or escape from the slot.

In an autonomous configuration, a person can:

- distinguish affective certainty from evidence;
- notice shame, disgust, or group pressure as signals rather than proofs;
- compare inherited classifications with direct experience;
- tolerate disagreement and temporary dissonance;
- question the authority that supplied a classification;
- revise the mythological lens when its classifications contradict evidence or experience.

Every installed program governs classification, and a lifted program necessarily
has meta-authority over first-order jurisdictions. **Pathological capture** begins
not with governing authority as such but when the weave or its representatives
become uncorrectable, monopolize authorized interpretation, exceed their
constituted jurisdiction, or claim **epistemic sovereignty**—that reality must
answer to authorization rather than authorization to reality. The bigOther then
determines which perceptions, questions, relationships, and feelings the main CPU
may treat as legitimate. Intelligence remains available, but is recruited to
implement or defend the installed lens.

Full capture can integrate an already compatible package, but capture by an
incompatible governing worldview requires replacement. Authorities, sanctions,
rituals, relationships, and field back-action must loosen the incumbent weave's
load-bearing bindings, make detachment or reinterpretation survivable, and help
rebraid one successor. Conversely, counter-relations and reality contact can
protect or repair the incumbent weave without acting as a second evaluator. A
rival successor is not pathological merely because it replaces an incumbent; its
corrigibility and authority relations must be assessed independently.

Susceptibility to such capture is not treated as a single scalar trait. It can involve different degrees of:

- programmability through narratives and exemplars;
- sensitivity to approval, rejection, and unanimity;
- ease of synchronization with a group;
- identity dependence;
- affective charge and internalization;
- resistance to counterevidence;
- difficulty of reprogramming;
- practical ability to dissent or leave.

### 17. Synonyms and terminological neighbours of the bigOther

There is no established term that is an exact synonym for the **bigOther** as culture mechanics defines it. The term deliberately combines a local sentiment classifier, its culturally learned programming, its relation to the symbolic order, and its capacity for distributed synchronization. Existing concepts usually name only one of these aspects.

The closest everyday term is **conscience** (*Gewissen*), provided that it is understood as an affectively active moral evaluator rather than as a little rational judge inside the mind. Conscience marks conduct and even contemplated conduct with approval, guilt, shame, obligation, or prohibition. In that respect it performs part of the bigOther's sentiment-classification function.

Conscience is nevertheless narrower than the bigOther. The bigOther also classifies status, belonging, legitimacy, loyalty, sacredness, pollution, identity, and social danger, including cases that are not ordinarily described as matters of conscience. Conversely, conscience can include deliberate moral reflection performed by the main CPU, whereas the bigOther refers specifically to the fast culturally programmed evaluator that influences such reflection. Culture mechanics can therefore distinguish a **socially authored conscience**, whose judgments are largely supplied by the surrounding field, from a more **self-authored conscience**, whose inherited classifications can be examined and deliberately endorsed, rejected, or revised.

| Neighbouring term | Main overlap with the bigOther | Important difference |
|---|---|---|
| **Lacan's big Other** | The prior symbolic order of language, law, roles, authority, and socially intelligible meaning; functionally, the shared protocol through which local states become publicly legible. | It is the source term, but Lacan did not describe a literal local co-processor, PCI card, or the full on-the-wire analogy. Culture mechanics adds that implementation and translation. |
| **James's felt objective presence** | A reality or unseen order may be experienced as objectively present and conduct-directing. | It illuminates **bigOther-presence** phenomenologically but does not specify its local processor, cultural program, or network mechanism. |
| **Buber's I–Thou relation** | Another is encountered in a second-person relational mode rather than as an object. | It illuminates **bigOther-as-Thou** but must not be reduced to social programming; Buber's eternal Thou is not the culture-mechanical bigOther. |
| **Conscience / Gewissen** | Internal moral approval, guilt, shame, obligation, and prohibition. | It is the closest ordinary-language partial synonym, but is narrower than the bigOther and can also include reflective moral judgment by the CPU. |
| **Superego** | Internalized authority, prohibitions, ideals, guilt, and demands placed upon the self. | It emphasizes a psychoanalytic intrapsychic agency, often especially its punitive aspect; it does not by itself denote the full symbolic network or distributed computation. |
| [**Generalized other**](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Mead/pubs2/mindself/Mead_1934_34.html) | George Herbert Mead's organized attitudes and expectations of a community that a person learns to take toward the self. | It closely describes the social standpoint represented locally, but not the proposed dual-processor architecture or its primitive affective interface. |
| **Collective conscience / collective consciousness** | Durkheim's shared beliefs and sentiments, with their moral authority over group members. | It is closest to the distributed bigOther; it does not isolate the local sentiment-classification unit through which collective sentiments become personally operative. |
| **Internalized norms, moral schemas, and moral identity** | Learned evaluative rules, expectations, and identity commitments. | These are components of the mythological lens executed by the bigOther rather than names for the processor, the narrative corpus, and the network together. |
| **Habitus** | Social history embodied as durable perception, feeling, and action dispositions. | It is broader than moral-social classification and does not posit a single-slot evaluator or a separate general-purpose CPU. |
| **Socialized mind** and **ego development** | Describe developmental relations to social expectations, internal standards, and one's own meaning-making system. | They describe organizations or levels of the whole person, not an additional processor. They are developmental neighbours of the bigOther, not synonyms for it. |

The vocabulary should therefore be used with the following rule:

> **Conscience, superego, generalized other, collective conscience, felt objective presence, and I–Thou relation each illuminate an aspect of the bigOther; none alone names the complete culture-mechanical construct.**

### 18. Development of the bigOther: Kegan and Loevinger

Culture mechanics treats the bigOther as developmentally variable. A person is not born with a completed mythological lens. Social evaluation is first encountered through attachment, imitation, direct reward and punishment, and dependence on caregivers. It is then internalized as group-authored conscience and may later become available for self-authorship and reflective revision.

[Robert Kegan's constructive-developmental theory](https://www.mindsatwork.com/our-thinking/how-adults-grow) and [Jane Loevinger's ego-development research](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED078066.pdf) provide the closest developmental models found in the surrounding conversations, but neither proposed a bigOther. Kegan's subject-object theory describes increasingly complex **forms of mind** or **meaning-making**, not merely moral conscience. Loevinger's theory describes development of the **ego** across impulse control, character, interpersonal understanding, conscious preoccupations, and moral evaluation. The culture-mechanical model below is consequently a synthesis and translation, not a claim that either theorist described the wireless collar.

The key developmental question is:

> **Is the mythological lens something the person can look at and revise, or is it the unquestioned lens through which the person looks?**

This is Kegan's subject-object distinction applied specifically to cultural sentiment classification. What remains **subject** to a person has the person: it organizes experience but cannot yet be examined as an object. What becomes **object** can be noticed, compared, evaluated, and acted upon. Development of the bigOther therefore does not primarily mean that the collar becomes stronger. It means that the homo-duplex system gains a more complex and reflective relation to the lens stored in the collar.

[*Epistemic Agency under Mechanical
Solidarity*](0020-epistemic-agency.semantic-definition.md#reflective-roles-of-reason-press-secretary-interpreter-and-auditor)
operationalizes this movement by distinguishing reason as **press secretary**,
**interpreter**, and **auditor**. Making a classification and its provenance
visible is the interpretive step; testing its framing, authority, jurisdiction,
evidence, consequence, and corrigibility is the further audit. Articulate reasons
alone do not establish that the main CPU authored the verdict.

#### A four-phase culture-mechanical model

| Culture-mechanical phase | Closest Keganian neighbour | Closest Loevingerian neighbour | Relation to the bigOther |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Installation and external regulation** | Impulsive and instrumental forms of mind | Impulsive and Self-Protective stages | Social control is experienced mainly through immediate attachment, advantage, reward, retaliation, and punishment. A stable moral-social lens is still being installed; much of its authority remains visibly outside the person. |
| **2. Socially authored bigOther** | Socialized Mind | Conformist and transitional Self-Aware levels | Group expectations, authorities, relationships, and accepted roles become internally binding. The person can subordinate impulse to shared rules, but has difficulty standing apart from the social field that defines what ought to feel admirable, shameful, loyal, or forbidden. Borrowed judgment is experienced as one's own obvious conscience. |
| **3. Self-authored bigOther** | Self-Authoring Mind | Conscientious stage | The person constructs an internal code and can use it to evaluate competing external demands. The main CPU can identify, endorse, and deliberately revise at least some mappings in the installed lens. The bigOther remains present, but its lens is increasingly governed from within rather than continuously authored by the current group. |
| **4. Self-transforming bigOther** | Self-Transforming Mind | Individualistic transition, Autonomous stage, and the rare Integrated stage | The person can take even a self-authored value system as object, recognize its partial and constructed character, tolerate conflicts among frameworks, and revise the meta-rules by which classifications are made. The lens can include its own corrigibility without dissolving all commitments. |

These are correspondences, not one-to-one equations. In particular, Loevinger's **Conscientious** stage is not simply “having a conscience”; it is a comparatively developed organization characterized by self-evaluated standards, responsibility, long-term goals, and evaluation of rules rather than mere acceptance of group rules. Her **Individualistic** level is a transition from Conscientious to Autonomous, and the Autonomous and Integrated levels add tolerance of inner conflict, ambiguity, interdependence, and increasingly integrated identity. Kegan describes a related shift in different terms: from being authored by relationships and the surrounding culture, to authoring an internal system, to being able to inspect the limitations of that system itself.

#### Development under the single-slot principle

Development does not install additional bigOthers beside the first one. The
computer still has one PCI slot, one operative sentiment-classification
architecture, and therefore one fully occupied slot. What changes is the person's
relation to the installed card and, during lifting, the order of the rules it runs:

```text
EXTERNALLY ENFORCED LENS
the environment supplies immediate reward and punishment
                    ↓
SOCIALLY AUTHORED LENS
the group's judgments operate internally as obvious conscience
                    ↓
SELF-AUTHORED LENS
the person can evaluate social demands through an internal code
                    ↓
SELF-TRANSFORMING LENS
the person can inspect and revise even the code and its meta-rules
```

A self-transforming person can represent several moral systems in the main CPU,
translate between them, and understand each from within. This does not require
several PCI cards running independently or a higher evaluator that ranks them at
runtime. Until reconstruction occurs, one local bigOther still executes one
operative lens. The developmental achievement is that the person can treat this
present lens as partial, corrigible, and open to deliberate repair, compatible
extension, unbraiding, or replacement.

Self-transformation can therefore unbraid a first-order program whose concrete
answers are treated as final and rebraid one lifted program in which binding
meta-rules generate, select, apply, and revise concrete answers. First-order
religious, professional, familial, political, or local packages need not vanish;
their particular rules and conclusions remain within the successor weave as
outputs or bounded packages answerable to its generator. This is full lifted
capture, not a neutral evaluator standing above culture.

Nor does greater autonomy require disconnection from distributed computing. The socially authored person is especially liable to be synchronized by whichever reference group carries authority. The self-authoring person can choose commitments and networks from an internal standpoint. The self-transforming person can participate in collective life without assuming that any single network exhausts reality, and can sometimes translate between networks without simply fusing with either one.

#### The “less than 1%” claim

The recollection that the most developed Keganian form occurs in fewer than one percent of adults is supported, with an attribution caveat. A peer-reviewed account by Lars Bauger, Rob Bongaardt, and Jack J. Bauer reports that, **according to Kegan and Lahey (2009)**, approximately 80–90% of adults make meaning between the Socialized and Self-Authoring levels, while the Self-Transforming Mind is “seldom seen (less than 1%).” The estimate is therefore appropriately attributed to **Kegan and Lahey's framework**, while the directly checkable wording appears in [Bauger, Bongaardt, and Bauer's 2021 article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10902-020-00274-0).

This figure should be treated as a reported developmental estimate, not as a population census, a biological constant, or a direct measurement of the bigOther. It concerns Kegan's highest whole-person meaning-making structure. Culture mechanics translates that structure into the capacity to take one's own mythological lens as object; it does not claim that Kegan measured a collar processor or that exactly the same percentage applies across all societies and methods.

#### Developmental cautions

Developmental complexity is not identical to intelligence, factual correctness, kindness, mental health, or moral worth. A powerful CPU does not guarantee a self-authored relation to the bigOther. Conversely, a self-authored person can construct and defend a cruel or epistemically contaminated code. Development describes increasing capacity to author and inspect a framework; the content and consequences of that framework must still be evaluated independently.

The highest phase should also not be understood as the disappearance of conscience. Its defining feature is **reflective ownership without finalization**: the person can retain commitments, feel moral sentiments, and participate in a culture while knowing that the framework producing those classifications is neither infallible nor complete.

### 19. Cross-references to closely related concepts

The following concepts overlap with parts of culture mechanics without being synonyms for the theory as a whole.

#### Affect Control Theory and BayesACT

**Affect Control Theory** (ACT) and its probabilistic extension **BayesACT** provide a formal computational neighbour to the culture-mechanical account of the mythological lens. ACT models culturally learned affective meanings—called **fundamental sentiments**—attached to identities, actions, and settings. An observed social event produces a **transient impression**, which is compared with these culturally expected meanings. The resulting discrepancy is called **deflection**. Deflection generates pressure toward emotion, reinterpretation, identity adjustment, or corrective action that restores affective consistency.

The correspondence is:

| Culture mechanics | ACT/BayesACT |
|---|---|
| **Mythological lens** | Culturally learned fundamental sentiments and impression-formation rules. |
| **Real-world observation or event** | An observed actor–behaviour–object event producing a transient impression. |
| **bigOther classification** | Comparison of the transient meaning with culturally expected meaning. |
| **Affective signal** | Confirmation or deflection—the discrepancy between expected and observed meaning. |
| **Resulting response** | Emotion, reinterpretation, identity adjustment, or corrective action. |

[BayesACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.5279) adds uncertain beliefs about identities and situations, Bayesian updating, explicit goals, and decision-theoretic action selection. In culture-mechanical terms, it offers one possible formalization of how an installed cultural mapping could combine with an observation to produce affective confirmation or error and consequent pressure on feeling and action:

```text
culturally learned affective expectations
                    +
          observed social event
                    ↓
      affective comparison or inference
                    ↓
          confirmation or deflection
                    ↓
        feeling and pressure to respond
```

This is a cross-reference rather than a foundation of culture mechanics. ACT and BayesACT do not propose a separate bigOther, a single PCI slot, mythological narrative programming, or distributed synchronization. They also do not explain how mythologies initially form the mythological lens. BayesACT's instrumental action selection extends beyond the primitive sentiment-classification function assigned to the collar and overlaps with planning functions that culture mechanics assigns to the main CPU.

BayesACT should not be conflated with the project's separate
[**auxiliary-un-reality explaining-away
cascade**](0030-epistemic-constitution.semantic-definition.md#auxiliary-un-reality-explaining-away-cascade).
That construct borrows the form of Bayesian *explaining away* as a diagnostic
contrast: independently evidenced alternative causes belong to legitimate model
revision, whereas the project-labelled counterfeit form treats an authorized
auxiliary account as if authorization were causal evidence and uses it to protect
an operative un-real representation. The social system need not calculate an
explicit probability, and neither Bayesian inference nor BayesACT is thereby
classified as un-reason.

#### Stanovich's rationality framework

Keith E. Stanovich, Richard F. West, and Maggie E. Toplak distinguish rationality from intelligence and describe cognition with a **tripartite model**:

- The **autonomous mind** produces rapid, automatic, highly practised, or evolutionarily prepared responses.
- The **algorithmic mind** supplies computational capacity for sustained processing, inhibition, and cognitive simulation. Conventional intelligence tests primarily track capacities at this level.
- The **reflective mind** concerns goals, epistemic values, thinking dispositions, and the decision to initiate reflective processing or override a default response.

This tripartite division should not be mapped one-to-one onto homo duplex. In culture mechanics, all three Stanovichian minds describe operations performed within the ordinary brain—the main cognitive system represented by the CPU, elephant, and rider. The bigOther is an additional functional distinction: a culturally programmable sentiment classifier coupled to that system.

The most faithful architectural mapping is:

| Stanovichian term | Function in Stanovich's model | Closest culture-mechanical location |
|---|---|---|
| **Autonomous mind** | Generates fast default responses | Part of the main brain and especially the elephant; it is influenced by, but is not identical to, the bigOther. |
| **Algorithmic mind** | Supplies computational power, decoupling, inhibition, and simulation | The processing power of the main CPU. |
| **Reflective mind** | Establishes goals and epistemic dispositions and initiates override | The governing and self-corrective activity of the main CPU, represented partly by the rider. |
| **Mindware** | Provides learned knowledge, rules, procedures, and strategies for reasoning | General acquired programming available to the main CPU; mythologies may carry mindware, while their culturally evaluative lessons can be compiled into the bigOther's mythological lens. |

This prevents two misleading equations:

> The autonomous mind is **not** the bigOther.  
> Mindware in general is **neither a mythology nor the mythological lens**.

Probability theory, falsification, causal reasoning, and decision rules are mindware used by the main CPU without necessarily belonging to the bigOther. A mythology is a culturally evaluated story and may carry many kinds of mindware. The mythological lens is narrower than mindware in general: it is the mapping distilled from many mythologies that organizes social identity and sentiment classification.

#### Mindware gaps and other rationality failures

A **mindware gap** exists when a person lacks a learned rule or body of knowledge required for rational judgment. Someone may possess ample algorithmic capacity but not understand base rates, regression to the mean, falsification, or probabilistic reasoning. In culture-mechanical language, the main CPU is powerful enough, but lacks the corrective program needed to evaluate or override a default response.

Stanovich's taxonomy separates this from several other failures:

| Failure | Stanovichian description | Culture-mechanical translation |
|---|---|---|
| **Cognitive miserliness** | Reflective processing is not initiated; an easy default is accepted. | The CPU does not spend the effort needed to inspect the elephant's response or the collar's classification. |
| **Override failure** | Reflective processing begins and relevant mindware exists, but the default response is not successfully suppressed. | The CPU detects a problem but fails to overcome the elephant's or bigOther's strongly weighted response. |
| **Mindware gap** | The alternative knowledge or rule needed for a rational response is absent or insufficiently learned. | The CPU lacks the epistemic tool needed to correct the response. |
| **Contaminated mindware** | Acquired mindware itself causes irrational belief or action. | In the culture-mechanical use developed here, the bigOther executes a defective lens that generates misdirecting sentiments and protects its mappings from evaluation. |

These failures can combine. A contaminated bigOther may discourage the reflective mind from initiating inspection, make affective override more difficult, and prevent missing corrective mindware from being learned.

#### Contaminated mindware as a badly programmed bigOther

In Stanovich's framework, **contaminated mindware** is acquired mindware that directly causes irrational belief or action—either by reducing epistemic accuracy or by thwarting effective goal pursuit. It can include superstitious thinking, antiscience attitudes, conspiracy beliefs, dysfunctional personal beliefs, misleading causal models, and culturally conditioned misinformation.

Stanovich gives special attention to mindware containing **evaluation-disabling properties**. Such mindware keeps itself lodged in a host by attaching punishment to questioning, rewarding unquestioning acceptance, or making itself unfalsifiable. This aspect provides the strongest direct bridge to culture mechanics because punishment, reward, and protected sacredness are operations of the bigOther's sentiment-classification system.

Following the present theory's guidance, culture mechanics adopts the term in this more specific functional sense:

> **Contaminated mindware is a badly programmed bigOther: contaminated mythologies have shaped a defective mythological lens whose classifications attach strong sentiments to thoughts and evidence in ways that systematically misguide the main CPU.**

The defective lens may mark:

- a false claim as sacred truth;
- honest doubt as betrayal;
- reliable evidence as enemy contamination;
- asymmetric scrutiny as moral vigilance;
- cruelty as righteous duty;
- correction as humiliation or surrender;
- loyalty to a source as a substitute for evaluating its claims.

The contamination lies in both **content** and **control**. Individual mythologies can carry misleading claims, roles, causal explanations, or anti-evaluation procedures. Repetition compiles those lessons into a defective lens, while the bigOther executes the lens and thereby determines how entertaining, questioning, revising, or rejecting them feels. Pride, shame, sacredness, belonging, authority, disgust, fear, and anticipated punishment protect the defective lens.

The resulting causal sequence is:

```text
CONTAMINATED BIGOTHER PROGRAMMING
misleading mythologies + evaluation-disabling procedures
                    ↓
DEFECTIVE MYTHOLOGICAL LENS
misdirected mappings from observations to values and feelings
                    ↓
STRONG AFFECTIVE MISCLASSIFICATION
false feels sacred, doubt feels disloyal,
harm feels righteous, correction feels shameful
                    ↓
REFLECTIVE INHIBITION OR FAILED OVERRIDE
the CPU does not inspect, cannot overcome, or lacks corrective mindware
                    ↓
MOTIVATED OR MYSIDE REASONING
selective search, asymmetric scrutiny, rationalization, and defence
                    ↓
IRRATIONAL BELIEF OR ACTION
reduced epistemic accuracy or frustrated goal attainment
```

The phrase “wrong direction” should be tied to Stanovich's standards of rationality rather than merely to disagreement with an observer. A bigOther is contaminated when its lens systematically impairs:

- **epistemic rationality:** forming beliefs that are appropriately calibrated to evidence and reality; or
- **instrumental rationality:** acting effectively in pursuit of the person's considered goals.

Political, religious, or moral disagreement alone is not evidence of contaminated mindware. The diagnosis requires such features as demonstrable epistemic failure, goal-thwarting consequences, asymmetric standards, or procedures that disable evaluation.

#### The extension and its boundary

The synchronization can now be stated precisely:

- **Stanovich supplies the cognitive pathology:** harmful acquired knowledge and strategies can cause irrationality even when computational intelligence is adequate.
- **Culture mechanics assigns that pathology a location in its dual architecture:** when contaminated mindware is cultural, identity-laden, and affectively enforced, it becomes part of a defective mythological lens executed by the bigOther.
- **Culture mechanics adds a causal interface:** the contaminated bigOther misguides the main CPU by attaching strong feelings to its thoughts and possible conclusions.
- **Culture mechanics adds a distributed mechanism:** networked bigOthers reward, punish, repeat, and synchronize contaminated mythologies and lens mappings across people.
- **Motivated or myside reasoning is usually downstream:** the main CPU is recruited to protect the direction already weighted by the bigOther.

This is a faithful extension, not a claim that Stanovich proposed a bigOther, a wireless collar, homo duplex, or distributed cultural computation. His category is broader: contaminated mindware need not always be socially synchronized or strongly affective. Culture mechanics deliberately concentrates on the subset for which the acquired contamination has entered the mythological lens of the sentiment classifier.

Conversely, not every mythology produces contaminated mindware, and no single story normally determines an entire lens. Mythologies can instead teach intellectual honesty, falsifiability, evidential symmetry, uncertainty, and willingness to revise. An open culture can thereby form a lens that makes reality-oriented conduct feel honourable and correction tolerable rather than misdirecting the CPU away from them.

#### Dysrationalia

**Dysrationalia** is Stanovich's term for irrational thought or behaviour despite adequate intelligence. It is closely related to the homo-duplex thesis because it rejects the assumption that a powerful main CPU guarantees rational judgment.

Culture mechanics proposes one possible social-affective pathway to dysrationalia: the bigOther can set priorities, prohibit questions, or make accurate revision socially painful while leaving general intelligence intact. Dysrationalia is nevertheless broader. It can also result from mindware gaps, cognitive miserliness, poor thinking dispositions, or failures to initiate reflective processing.

#### Motivated reasoning

**Motivated reasoning** is the tendency for desired conclusions, identities, loyalties, or emotional needs to influence how evidence is sought, interpreted, remembered, and evaluated.

In culture mechanics, motivated reasoning is not itself the bigOther. It is what the main CPU may do after the bigOther has marked one conclusion as honourable, safe, identity-preserving, or socially rewarded and another as shameful, dangerous, or disloyal. The CPU's power can then increase the sophistication of the rationalization rather than correct its direction.

Not every instance of reasoning from one's existing perspective is irrational. Prior beliefs may deserve evidential weight, and revising an established model has real cognitive costs. The culture-mechanical concern is the narrower case in which the bigOther enforces asymmetric or evaluation-disabling treatment of evidence and thereby reduces epistemic or instrumental rationality.

When that directional use becomes recursively organized so that concepts,
premises, burdens, exceptions, and even criticism are made to protect the installed
verdict from possible defeat, [*Epistemic Constitution*](0030-epistemic-constitution.semantic-definition.md#un-reason-reasoning-that-defeats-the-conditions-of-reason)
calls the mechanism **un-reason**. It explains how the main processor's
sophistication can strengthen capture through reasoning itself. Motivated
reasoning is one possible contributor; un-reason is the wider cross-scale
organization and does not follow from intelligence, disagreement, or one error.

#### Identity-protective cognition

**Identity-protective cognition** is motivated reasoning specifically directed toward protecting membership, status, or alignment within an identity-defining group. It is one of the closest empirical neighbours of the wireless-collar model.

The bigOther supplies an internalized audience and asks, in effect:

> What would accepting this evidence say about who I am, whose side I am on, and whether I still belong?

The person may consequently reject an accurate conclusion not because it exceeds the main CPU's capacity, but because accepting it would trigger shame, loss of status, or symbolic expulsion.

#### Moral conviction, sacred values, and taboo trade-offs

**Moral conviction** occurs when a preference or belief is experienced as a matter of fundamental right and wrong rather than negotiable interest. A **sacred value** is protected from ordinary comparison or exchange. A **taboo trade-off** is a comparison whose mere consideration is treated as morally offensive.

These concepts describe classifications produced by the bigOther when it assigns exceptional moral authority to an object or rule. They help explain why compromise can feel like corruption and why cost-benefit reasoning can provoke outrage rather than deliberation. They also distinguish an ordinary strong preference from programming that determines what the main CPU is permitted even to compare.

#### Actively open-minded thinking and epistemic autonomy

**Actively open-minded thinking** is the disposition to search for reasons one may be wrong, consider alternatives, apply standards symmetrically, and revise beliefs in response to evidence. It is a form of healthy mindware and a partial operational counterpart to what culture mechanics calls **epistemic autonomy**.

Epistemic autonomy does not require the absence of a bigOther. It requires a mythological lens that permits the main CPU to inspect the bigOther's outputs and that does not convert doubt, correction, or uncertainty automatically into shame and betrayal.

Actively open-minded thinking predicts resistance to many reasoning errors and forms of contaminated mindware, but it should not be treated as a universal antidote. Stanovich's work finds myside bias unusually resistant to prediction by intelligence and, in several paradigms, by actively open-minded-thinking measures themselves.

#### Master signifiers

In Lacanian terminology, a **master signifier** is a privileged signifier that organizes other meanings and identifications. Words such as *Nation*, *Justice*, *Science*, *Tradition*, *Safety*, *Freedom*, or *Revolution* can function in this way.

A master signifier is not inherently contaminated. It becomes a carrier of contaminated mindware when invoking it terminates evaluation rather than organizing an inquiry—for example, when “Science” changes from the name of a corrigible method into an authority token that makes further judgment impermissible.

In the wireless-collar and protocol analogies, a master signifier is a **highly
compressed protocol token**. It indexes a large package of already installed
mythologies, classifications, authorities, identities, sentiments, and expected
conduct without restating them. This explains both its efficiency and its danger:
participants can coordinate through a short token, but the expansion it activates
may remain unspoken and therefore difficult to inspect.

##### Master-signifier analysis grid

> **A master signifier should be analysed by what it sacralizes, who bears it,
> how it unifies, what it authorizes, and what happens to the outlier—not merely
> by its dictionary meaning.**

The **master-signifier analysis grid** is a project-developed decompression
method. The assistant synthesized the grid from the Humanism–Diversity
discussion; the project author then selected it for theory integration. It
compares the operative structures activated by a signifier without turning those
structures into one scalar judgment:

| Field | Diagnostic question |
|---|---|
| **Token family** | Which word, capitalization, slogan, symbol, negation, and neighbouring terms select the same installed complex? |
| **Sacred referent** | Which person, relation, collective, abstraction, pattern, process, condition, or future, if any, is placed beyond ordinary trade-off? |
| **Bearer** | Who or what, if anything, is said to instantiate, represent, carry, embody, or transmit the sacred referent? |
| **Protected principal** | Whose or what interests, standing, continuity, or development, if any, finally select action when goods conflict? |
| **Unity topology** | Does the signifier join persons directly through a common standing, mediate membership through groups, federate differentiated forms, or impose one higher-order content upon them? |
| **Difference operator** | Which distinctions are ignored, protected, amplified, administered, ranked, corrected, or treated as suspect? |
| **Distributive grammar** | What is allocated—standing, liberty, voice, office, recognition, burden, remedy, resources, reward, opportunity, or outcome—and by which rule: equality, contribution, need, injury, category, parity, or another principle? |
| **Causal story** | Which condition becomes a problem, who or what caused it, who is harmed or blamed, and which fixer and remedy follow? |
| **Interpreter custody** | Who defines the term, its bearers, categories, indicators, exceptions, conflicts, and legitimate application? Can rival interpretations defeat the custodian's account? |
| **Authorization and jurisdiction** | Which power, objects, domain, duration, discretion, sanction, and epistemic authority does invocation support? |
| **Outlier performance** | What happens to the difficult case—the infant, dependant, dissenter, noncontributor, mixed or unclassified person, statistical minority, failed exemplar, or person whose concrete interest conflicts with the aggregate or pattern? |
| **Realization stack** | How does the signifier operate semantically, affectively, behaviourally, institutionally, and materially rather than only propositionally? |
| **Completion** | What observable condition permits the demand, correction, emergency, or programme to count as complete? |
| **Correction** | Which evidence, consequence, appeal, refusal, succession, or external reality check can revise the interpretation and authorization? |
| **Trajectory** | Across time, does the complex build and transmit finite goods, stabilize bounded maintenance, renew its own demand, displace its principal, or consume its carriers and containing order? |

**Outlier performance** is not a licence to stigmatize unusual persons. It is a
stress test of what the signifier actually protects when its easiest representative
case is unavailable. **Distributive grammar** likewise does not presume that one
rule fits every object: equal moral standing, contribution-sensitive reward,
need-based aid, compensation for a demonstrated injury, and role-specific
authority answer different questions.

The grid is neither a new constitutional orientation nor a diagnostic score.
Some master signifiers organize knowledge or coordination without one sacred
referent, bearer, or protected principal; the grid records that absence rather
than manufacturing one.
Several fields deliberately cross-reference independent theory components:
causal stories and authorization payloads, subjecthood absorption, truth and
drive orientations, Culture Program jurisdiction, and service direction. A field
should be retained only if comparative application changes or sharpens the
analysis. A master signifier remains capable of healthy compression when its
referent, custody, authorization, boundaries, completion, and correction can be
made explicit and contested.

When *Science*, *Safety*, *biodiversity*, *risk*, or another master signifier
compresses a model-mediated authorization, the grid should be paired with the
[**model-to-mandate custody
chain**](0040-mythology-construction.semantic-definition.md#model-to-mandate-custody-chain).
That chain does not add another grid axis. It decompresses the existing causal
story, interpreter custody, authorization, completion, and correction fields into
observation, metric, model, conditional forecast, harm translation,
protected-principal valuation, severity threshold, remedy comparison, mandate,
and outcome audit. A valid early stage may coexist with an invalid later crossing,
and a disputed mandate does not by itself falsify the underlying observation or
model.

#### Bonhoeffer's Dummheit

Dietrich Bonhoeffer's 1943 reflection [“Von der Dummheit” (PDF of the
complete section)](https://jochenteuffel.files.wordpress.com/2020/12/bonhoeffer-von-der-dummheit.pdf)
is the starting point of the culture-mechanics theory. It posed the originating
questions: **What causes this *Dummheit*? How does it work?**

The German original reads:

> Jede starke äußere Machtentfaltung, sei sie politischer oder religiöser Art,
> schlägt einen großen Teil der Menschen mit Dummheit.
> Ja, es hat den Anschein, als sei das geradezu ein soziologisch-psychologisches
> Gesetz.
> Die Macht der einen braucht die Dummheit der anderen.
> Der Vorgang ist dabei nicht der, dass bestimmte – also etwa intellektuelle –
> Anlagen des Menschen plötzlich verkümmern oder ausfallen,
> sondern dass unter dem überwältigenden Eindruck der Machtentfaltung dem Menschen
> seine innere Selbständigkeit geraubt wird und dass dieser nun – mehr oder weniger
> unbewusst – darauf verzichtet,
> zu den sich ergebenden Lebenslagen ein eigenes Verhalten zu finden. Dass der

The English translation used here is:

> Every powerful display of external force, whether political or religious,
> strikes a large portion of people with stupidity.
> Indeed, it appears to be almost a sociological-psychological law: the power of
> the few needs the stupidity of the many.
> The process does not consist of certain intellectual faculties suddenly
> withering.
> Rather, under the overwhelming impression of power, people are robbed of their
> inner independence and - more or less unconsciously - give up forming their own
> judgments about the situations that arise.
> The fact that the stupid person is often obstinate should not hide the reality
> that he is not acting autonomously.
> **In conversation one senses that one is not dealing with the person himself but
> with slogans and catch-phrases that have gained mastery over him.**
> **He is under a spell, blinded, his very being abused and misused.**
> Having become a will-less instrument, the stupid person is capable of any evil
> and at the same time incapable of recognizing it as evil.
> Here lies the danger of a diabolical misuse.

This is not a claim about low intelligence. Bonhoeffer describes a socially
induced loss of inner independence in which external power, slogans, and formulas
speak through a person whose ordinary intellectual abilities remain available.
His *Dummheit* is therefore broader and more political than contaminated mindware.

Bonhoeffer names the phenomenon; culture mechanics proposes a mechanism. At the
individual level, an intelligent person's main CPU can remain capable while the
bigOther supplies the classifications, slogans, sentiments, and permissions under
which that intelligence operates. [*Epistemic Agency under Mechanical
Solidarity*](0020-epistemic-agency.semantic-definition.md) develops the social-field

borrowed judgment feel internally compulsory and thereby weaken the person's
capacity to form an independent response.

At whole-polity scale, Bonhoeffer's power--*Dummheit* relation is the originating

Fascism](0020-epistemic-agency.semantic-definition.md#fascism-as-a-strong-whole-polity-realization).
Concentrated directing power cannot itself perceive, administer, reproduce, and

The resulting feedback strengthens the power whose field helped produce the

equal responsibility between rulers, carriers, and coerced participants.

#### Normopathie and political ponerology: two faces of captured culture

Culture mechanics treats **Normopathie** and **political ponerology** as complementary perspectives on a captured cultural system. They are not identical concepts. They describe the same pathological configuration from different social positions:

- **Normopathie** describes the population-side result. People over-adapt to a pathological social normality and cease to recognize it as pathological. Their local bigOthers defer to authorities, prestige sources, reference groups, and apparent consensus; borrowed classifications may then crystallize into moral emotions and eventually feel like an autonomous conscience.
- **Political ponerology** describes the formation and exploitation of destructive political power. Its concepts of **spellbinders**, ponerogenic processes, and **pathocracy** provide the more precise elite-side vocabulary: manipulative, characterologically destructive, or cynically instrumental actors create, capture, intensify, administer, and profit from a pathological cultural order.

The two faces belong to one culture-mechanical “coin”: a **captured distributed bigOther**. On one face are the people living under its spell; on the other are the spellbinders and seducers who shape, exploit, and gain power from it.

```text
SPELLBINDERS AND PATHOCRATIC ACTORS
select and manipulate mythologies, symbols, systems, and sanctions
                         ↓
CAPTURED MYTHOLOGICAL LENS / DISTRIBUTED BIGOTHER
defines normality, virtue, loyalty, danger, and deviance
                         ↓
NORMOPATHIC ADAPTATION
borrowed judgment, conformity, Dummheit, denunciation, self-policing
                         ↓
APPARENT CONSENSUS AND DISTRIBUTED ENFORCEMENT
reward the spellbinders and strengthen their control
                         ↺
```

Systems Leadership identifies important channels through which this capture can occur. Systems, symbols, leadership behaviour, and mythologies can be selected or modified so that the resulting mythological lens maps obedience to virtue, dissent to betrayal, dependence to safety, and elite interest to the common good. Political seduction then substitutes identification and affective trust for examination: the seducer offers protection, belonging, moral certainty, or relief from responsibility while seeking access, loyalty, power, and impunity.

The normopathically adapted population is therefore not merely passive. Once local bigOthers have been synchronized, ordinary participants become the distributed execution and enforcement layer of the captured bigOther. They repeat its mythologies, display its classifications, reward conformity, punish dissent, and manufacture the apparent consensus to which other uncertain people defer. A powerful main CPU may assist this process by producing sophisticated rationalizations for directions that the bigOther has already made emotionally compulsory.

The relation is recursive rather than simply top-down. Spellbinders gain a selection advantage in a culture whose lens rewards their methods, while their control over institutions enables them to strengthen that lens. A normopathic culture can arise without a single deliberate architect through decentralized conformity, dependency, and institutional inertia; it can then be captured by ponerological actors. Conversely, a would-be spellbinder cannot sustain a pathocracy without compatible institutions and a sufficiently synchronized population.

In this synthesis, Bonhoeffer's **Dummheit** names a characteristic loss of inner independence on the population side; **Normopathie** names the normalization and social reproduction of that condition; and political ponerology—especially **pathocracy**—names the elite formation and power process that can cultivate and exploit it.

These terms are interpretive cross-references, not interchangeable clinical diagnoses or licenses to pathologize political opponents. Normopathie and political ponerology are not standard mainstream empirical categories, and the culture-mechanical connection proposed here should not be attributed directly to Hans-Joachim Maaz or Andrzej M. Łobaczewski. Its claim is narrower: the concepts illuminate complementary positions within one possible cultural feedback loop.

#### Kant's inner tribunal and doubled personality

Immanuel Kant's account of conscience in [*The Metaphysics of Morals*, AA
6:438](https://korpora.org/kant/aa06/438.html) and [AA
6:439](https://www.korpora.org/Kant/aa06/439.html) supplies an independently
developed language for a moral phenomenology relevant to homo duplex and
bigOther-presence. It does not supply the Culture Mechanics processor or its
social explanation.

Kant calls conscience the consciousness of an **“inner court”** (*innerer
Gerichtshof*) in which a person's thoughts accuse or excuse one another. Every
person, he says, finds themselves:

- observed by an **inner judge** (*innerer Richter*);
- threatened and held in *Respect*, which Kant glosses as respect joined with
  fear;
- answerable to a power they did not make arbitrarily, but which is incorporated
  into their being;
- followed by that authority **“like his shadow”**;
- periodically awakened by its **“terrible voice”** even after trying to stupefy
  or distract themselves; and
- constrained to conduct this business with themselves **“as at the command of
  another person”** (*als auf den Geheiß einer anderen Person*).

The juridical structure creates a problem. If accused, accuser, and judge were
simply the same party in the same capacity, Kant argues, the prosecutor would
always lose. Conscience must therefore represent an **other**--an actual or ideal
person--as judge. At AA 6:439 Kant names the resulting structure:

> **“Die zwiefache Persönlichkeit ... dieses doppelte Selbst”**
>
> **“The doubled personality ... this double self.”**
>
> *(close project translation)*

The complete footnote is more differentiated than a simple “higher judge versus
lower accused” picture. The courtroom contains accuser, accused, legal counsel,
and judge. Kant identifies accuser and accused as the same human being *numero
idem* while distinguishing their practical capacities. As subject of a moral
legislation grounded in freedom, the human being is considered as *homo noumenon*
and as **another** relative to the rational sensible human being--*specie
diversus*, but only in a practical respect. The inner judge finally issues the
verdict whose authority reason can honour only as an unconditional *iubeo* or
*veto*.

Kant's affective account matters as much as the courtroom roles. In the [*Critique
of Practical Reason*, AA 5:73](https://korpora.org/kant/aa05/073.html), the moral
law strikes down and humiliates self-conceit while becoming an object of the
greatest respect. At [AA 5:80](https://korpora.org/kant/aa05/080.html), its voice
makes even the boldest transgressor tremble; respect combines freely submitting
one's will with an unavoidable constraint imposed by one's own reason and
therefore contains both displeasure and elevation. In the [*Groundwork*, AA
4:401](https://korpora.org/kant/aa04/401.html), respect is a feeling produced by a
concept of reason and the consciousness that the will is subordinated to a law
one imposes upon oneself.

This produces an exact comparative tension:

> **For conscience to be autonomous, its authority must be mine. For conscience
> to judge me, its authority must confront me as another.**

The following mapping keeps the shared explanatory target and the incompatible
explanations visible:

| Kantian formulation | Culture-mechanical interpretation | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Inner court, accuser, accused, counsel, and judge** | Moral evaluation can be lived as a structured encounter among functionally different positions inside one person | Kant's practical roles are not empirical processors or several local bigOthers |
| **Observed, threatened, and held in respect joined with fear** | Affective classification can make authority present, inescapable, and conduct-directing | Kant derives respect from practical reason; he does not explain it as social programming |
| **The authority was not made arbitrarily and follows like a shadow** | Installed authority can feel discovered or incorporated rather than voluntarily invented | For Kant this is an original moral predisposition, not evidence of a culturally installed lens |
| **Business with oneself conducted as at the command of another person** | A local moral authority can be encountered in the second-person form of bigOther-as-Thou | Kant's represented other may be actual or ideal and belongs to his practical-rational account, not the Culture Mechanics bigOther |
| **Doubled personality / double self** | An independent philosophical description of functional non-unitarity relevant to homo duplex | It is neither a clinical split personality nor an identity with the main-CPU/local-bigOther architecture |
| ***Homo noumenon* and the rational sensible human being** | The comparison invites separate analysis of the authorizing position and the person subject to affect, inclination, and consequence | *Homo noumenon* is not the local bigOther, and the sensible human being is not simply the main CPU |
| **Terrible voice and unconditional *iubeo* or *veto*** | Moral programming can become agentified, command-like, and phenomenologically authoritative | The ordinary local bigOther need not speak, personify itself, or produce auditory experience |

Kant, Durkheim, and Culture Mechanics offer different accounts of this overlapping
phenomenological target:

| Account | Source and authority of the moral encounter |
|---|---|
| **Kant** | Autonomous practical reason represents the ideal judge and gives itself universally binding moral law |
| **Durkheim** | Society's sacred moral ideal confronts the individual through collective beliefs and sentiments |
| **Culture Mechanics hypothesis** | A local bigOther executes a socially installed moral lens and can present its authority as an observing and judging Thou; a live collective field may amplify and renew that local operation |

The safe comparative formulation is:

> **Kant's *zwiefache Persönlichkeit* independently describes a moral
> phenomenology in which one numerically identical person occupies distinct
> practical positions and an internally rooted authority confronts the sensible
> person as an observing, judging other. Culture Mechanics models a structurally
> similar relation through homo duplex and bigOther-presence. Kant explains the
> authority as autonomous practical reason; Culture Mechanics hypothesizes a
> culturally programmed local bigOther. The comparison identifies a shared
> explanatory target, not a shared ontology or empirical confirmation.**

The comparison supports neither a historical claim nor an inference to collective
agency. A severe Kantian conscience could remain locally active after the culture
that formed or reinforced it had disappeared. Inner observation, command, guilt,
or awe establishes at most the phenomenology of local presence. A
**multi-mind-self** additionally requires a distributed system with sufficiently
integrated perception, memory, judgment, intention, ownership, learning, action,
and continuity. Explaining how a law becomes psychologically gripping would also
not by itself establish the law's moral validity.

#### Jaynes, Bach, and agentified collective enactment

[Julian Jaynes's *The Origin of Consciousness in the Breakdown of the Bicameral
Mind*](https://www.julianjaynes.org/book/ooc/en/the-bicameral-mind/) and Joscha

Jaynes hypothesized an older mentality in which action-guiding products were
experienced as the auditory commands of gods rather than as self-authored
deliberation. Bach describes selves and gods as implemented agents and develops
the possibility of a multi-mind-self enacted across several human minds. Culture
Mechanics does not adopt either complete theory. It uses their conjunction to ask
how a culturally represented agent can become locally present and distributedly
operative.

| Neighbouring proposal | Culture-mechanical interpretation | Boundary |

| **Bach's god or top-level agent implemented across minds** | A distributed bigOther that can produce a multi-mind-self presentation and may cross the further threshold into collective perception, memory, judgment, intention, ownership, and action | Shared representation and synchronized devotion alone remain below the operative multi-mind-self threshold. |
| **Human alignment and collective agency** | Coupling, compatible programming, functional integration, and collective-agent formation | Alignment is not one scalar and does not establish truth, goodness, jurisdiction, corrigibility, or fusion. |

The safe comparative formulation is:

> **Jaynesian bicameral mentality can be interpreted as a culturally produced,
> highly agentified, command-like operating mode of homo duplex in which the
> culturally programmed second side becomes phenomenologically present as one or
> more gods and receives action-guiding authority otherwise attributed to an
> autobiographical self.**

The less historically committed name **bicameral-like enactment** refers to that
possible regime without accepting Jaynes's dating, universal scope, philology, or
neurological explanation. Jaynes emphasizes intrapersonal phenomenology; Bach
emphasizes distributed implementation; Culture Mechanics proposes a functional
bridge:

```text
shared mythology of a god
→ god-agent represented in local mythological lenses
→ agentified Beseelung and presence as Thou
→ coordinated enactment through several persons and institutions
→ distributed bigOther
→ god-as-multi-mind-self presentation
→ possible operative god-as-multi-mind-self
→ social confirmation of the god's local presence
```

The god-agent can acquire functional perception through adherents, memory through
scripture and tradition, judgment through compatible local bigOthers, speech
through persons and institutions, intention through ritual or decision procedures,
ownership through sacred or common objects, and action through coordinated bodies.
This implementation can remain differentiated. It becomes fused when the
collectively enacted subject displaces participating persons as independently
legitimate authors.

A pantheon is compatible with the single-slot principle when its gods are
agent-personae with differentiated jurisdictions inside one operative weave. A
ritual or situation can activate one persona without changing the processor.
Incompatible top-level packages contesting the same load-bearing classifications
still require rejection or replacement under single-slot exclusivity.

These comparisons have strict evidential limits. A [multidisciplinary critical
reappraisal](https://www.julianjaynes.org/resources/articles/the-bicameral-mind-30-years-on-a-critical-reappraisal-of-julian-jaynes-hypothesis/)
identifies neurological and philological problems in Jaynes's proposal while
treating the non-unitary self as an important legacy. Ancient texts rarely decide
whether a reported god-voice was an auditory experience, ritual language,
literary convention, political legitimation, or some combination. Resemblance to
Jaynes or Bach therefore does not validate the local-bigOther architecture, prove
a history of consciousness, reduce theological beings to human artifacts, or make
every religion, ritual, shared intention, or synchronized group a multi-mind-self.

### 20. Scope and terminological cautions

*Culture mechanics* is a proposed synthetic term, not the established name of a single accepted scientific theory. It combines ideas associated with social psychology, Durkheimian sociology, Lacanian psychoanalysis, Systems Leadership, narrative theory, cybernetics, distributed cognition, cultural evolution, and systems theory.

Its computer and wireless-collar descriptions are functional models, not claims that the brain literally contains a PCI card or a separately identifiable anatomical collar processor. Its charged-particle, field, laser, wireless, and computing comparisons describe proposed relations and generate hypotheses; they do not assert that social processes are physically electromagnetic or obey the equations of the source domains.

Its use of **homo duplex** extends Durkheim. Its use of **bigOther** operationalizes and modifies Lacan. Its protocol interpretation isolates one functional aspect of Lacan's symbolic order rather than exhausting the concept. **bigOther-presence** and **bigOther-as-Thou** borrow phenomenological cues from James and Buber without attributing the proposed processor or field mechanism to either thinker. These inherited terms name important sources of the model but should not be mistaken for exact summaries of their doctrines.

**Relevant structure** and the underlying connection between experienced moral
good or evil and represented structural support or damage are adapted from Dushan
Wegner's *Relevante Strukturen*. The typed relational definition, the separation
of felt relevance, represented effect, actual effect, generative centrality, and
normative standing, the multidimensional account of strengthening, and the
placement of the appraisal inside the homo-duplex and single-slot architecture
are Culture Mechanics extensions. They should not be attributed to Wegner as his
own distinctions.
The named change-indexed moral-mobilization hypothesis is likewise a
project-developed operationalization of Wegner's emphasis on change, not his own
formal construct.

Kant's inner court, inner judge, doubled personality, double self, *homo
noumenon*, and affective account of respect are neighbouring philosophical
descriptions, not the Culture Mechanics architecture and not empirical
confirmation of it. Kant locates moral authority in autonomous practical reason,
not in a socially programmed local bigOther. The project comparison isolates a
shared phenomenological problem--an internally rooted authority confronting the
person as another--while retaining the conflict between their explanations.

Jaynes's bicameral mind and Bach's implemented multi-mind-self are neighbouring
theories, not analogies or empirical confirmation. The term *multi-mind-self* in
this project is taken from Bach. Culture Mechanics adds the distinction between a
multi-mind-self presentation, an operative multi-mind-self that crosses an
explicit functional threshold, and a fused multi-mind-self, together with the
family-level shorthand rule stated above. The bicameral-like comparison is narrower than Jaynes's historical theory
and must not be used to claim that ancient people universally lacked consciousness
or an individual mind.

The theory's three constitutive commitments each carry an explanatory burden. The
local bigOther is functionally distinct from the main CPU; the distributed field
is emergent from relations among persons, communications, institutions, practices,
and artifacts; and the single-slot principle permits a modular weave of compatible
packages while denying parallel operative evaluators. Under the third commitment,
first-order and lifted programming both fully occupy the slot; order level
distinguishes shared concrete answers from a shared generator of answers; a lifted
generator may govern universally while constituting limited subordinate
jurisdictions; and corrigibility must be evaluated independently. These claims
should produce comparative explanations and possible tests. None licenses an
immaterial substance, erases the responsibility and causal contribution of
biological persons, or proves that every apparent conflict of allegiance is a
completed worldview replacement.

The single-slot commitment is therefore defeasible in principle. A stable case of
two incompatible evaluators remaining independently final over the same conduct,
each generating its own affect, veto, and correction without hierarchy,
reinterpretation, contextual division, or a third governing rule, would count
against it. This defeat condition should not be neutralized after the fact by
calling every such observation a complex braid.

The framework also does not claim that cultural programming determines behaviour mechanically. Humans interpret, combine, resist, conceal, question, and change symbolic instructions. The model proposes structured influence, not perfect control.

**Culture-mechanical object**, **ideological symbol**, **conformity signal**,
**targeting signal**, the four propagation and enforcement forms, **jealous
cultural program**, **enforcement--exclusion duality**, **protection-framed
jurisdictional manoeuvre**, and the combined signal--moralization--jurisdiction
mechanism are project-developed constructs.
**Situated cultural script**, **script viability**, **script load-bearingness**,
and **cross-script cascade** are likewise project-developed Culture Mechanics
terms developed from Rao's neighbouring account of learned scripts, meaning,
material support, and unraveling. Rao does not supply the one-slot architecture,
the cultural-braid hierarchy, or the distinction between script repair and
governing-weave replacement.
The four forms are ideal types rather than a validated scale. Andrick supplies the
theory of moralization attributed to him; the processor, protocol, field, jealousy,
and ideology-as-hallucination extensions belong to Culture Mechanics. Whole
religion classifications and the Iran end-state claim are explicit hypotheses,
not facts established by the definitions themselves.
[Gregory H. Stanton](https://www.genocidewatch.com/tenstages) supplies the
neighbouring genocidal-process model attributed to him, not the targeting-signal
definition or the culture-mechanical causal ontology.

An observed symbol does not by itself disclose the carrier's private intention,
degree of belief, or complete program. The same token can be voluntary, coerced,
devotional, propagating, targeting, jurisdictional, resistant, or multiply
functional in different settings. Target status can be made legible through
performance, absence, imposition, or attribution. The relevant evidence includes
who is expected to perform the token, who assigns it, who may refuse, how absence
is interpreted, what sanctions follow, which jurisdiction is claimed, and what the
program does after acquiring power.

Finally, describing culture as programming does not imply that all symbolic reality is false. Money, law, office, obligation, legitimacy, and dignity are symbolically constituted but socially consequential realities. Culture mechanics distinguishes such constitutive social meanings from factual claims about physical reality, which remain answerable to evidence.

  interfaces, improvisations, and practical support.
- **Abstraction and execution:** the cultural braid is broader and more abstract;
  a script is narrower but more concretely specified; a local script variant is
  one implementation; an enactment is one performance, modification, or refusal.
- **Script viability and unraveling:** viability asks whether cultural meaning
  still generates intelligible and practically supportable conduct; unraveling is
  the mutually reinforcing loss of meaning-side and practical-side coherence.
  Neither establishes truth, legitimacy, flourishing, or slot vacancy.
- **Script propagation:** peripheral script failure may be repaired within the
  incumbent braid, while failure of load-bearing or mutually dependent scripts
  can create a cross-script cascade and contribute to cultural unbraiding.
- **Relevant structure:** a represented relational arrangement or organized
  condition that the installed lens treats as carrying moral weight.
- **Relevant-structure appraisal hypothesis:** people often experience represented
  support for a felt-relevant structure as good and represented damage as evil;
  felt relevance, represented effect, actual effect, generative centrality, and
  normative standing remain distinct.
- **Generative-centrality distinction:** a structure can causally produce
  capabilities required by many downstream structures without receiving
  corresponding felt relevance; a cultural sublimation architecture is one
  proposed case.
- **Generator–architecture distinction:** a lifted sublimation generator selects
  binding directions; actuation and cultural sublimation architecture make those
  directions effective and durable. Structural efficacy and positive value remain
  separate.
- **Change-indexed moral-mobilization hypothesis:** moral appraisal often becomes
  more salient and action-directing when a condition is represented as causing,
  continuing, preventing, or failing to prevent change in a felt-relevant
  structure; this is a testable mobilization pathway, not a complete ontology of
  ethics.
- **Analytical umbrella:** a culture-mechanical object is a program, carrier,
  implementation arrangement, processor state, collective formation, or
  collective agent involved in cultural installation, activation, transmission,
  execution, synchronization, or enforcement; its subtype must be identified.
- **Religion-inclusive program category:** religions, secular ideologies, company
  cultures, activist movements, professional cultures, and state programmes are
  analyzed as members of the same broad family rather than different ontological
  kinds.
- **Ideological symbol:** a medium-independent socially legible token that
  activates an installed program and communicates a person's relation to it.
- **Master-signifier analysis grid:** a non-scalar decompression method comparing
  sacred referent, bearer, protected principal, unity topology, difference
  operator, distributive grammar, causal story, interpreter custody,
  authorization, outlier performance, realization, completion, correction, and
  trajectory.
- **Conformity signal:** an ideological symbol whose performance, absence,
  refusal, or substitution makes conformity and deviation publicly legible.
- **Targeting signal:** a performed, omitted, imposed, or attributed socially
  legible token that assigns a person or population to a sanctionable out-group;
  an absent conformity signal can therefore become a target marker.
- **Propagation and enforcement forms:** private, collective, ideological form,
  and fundamentalist; these are ideal-type program configurations, not kinds of
  bigOther or one necessary developmental staircase.
- **Conflict ladder:** propositional, causal-story, and classification conflicts
  remain distinct from mythological rivalry; active interference creates
  mythological conflict, incompatible governing lenses create cultural-program
  conflict, organized recruitment and suppression create ideological-form
  conflict, and physical coercion or violence marks fundamentalist-form conflict.
- **Signal-suppression distinction:** rival-signal or counter-program suppression
  describes interference with opposition without analytically reducing rival or
  corrective signals to random noise.
- **Independent animation axis:** *Beseelung* describes how affectively alive and
  conduct-directing a program is, independently of its propagation or violence.
- **Jealous cultural program:** a program that claims exclusive allegiance and
  treats incompatible commitment, dissent, or exit as betrayal; single-slot
  exclusivity alone is not jealousy.
- **Moralized enforcement bridge:** a signal or refusal becomes character
  disclosure, character accusation attacks civic or social standing, solidarity
  distributes the judgment, and systems attach exclusion or coercion.
- **Enforcement--exclusion duality:** enforcement conditions continued inclusion,
  standing, access, or permitted action on conformity; exclusion is the withdrawal
  or denial applied to refusal, and one visible sanction can perform both operations.
- **Fundamentalist threshold:** authorization or willingness to use physical or
  eliminative force, not the mere presence of moral evaluation or discursive
  violence.
- **Genocidal escalation neighbour:** Stanton's ten overlapping processes map one
  possible path from classification and symbolization through discrimination,
  dehumanization, organization, polarization, preparation, persecution, and
  extermination to denial; they are not a universal ladder or a synonym for the
  broader culture-mechanical repertoire.
- **Local processor:** the bigOther that executes the lens.
- **Single-slot constraint:** one local bigOther executes one operative lens; no higher runtime evaluator ranks parallel bigOthers or governing worldviews.
- **Complete slot occupancy / full capture:** a stable first-order or lifted program completely occupies the one slot; *full* does not by itself mean pathological.
- **Order levels:** first-order mechanical solidarity shares concrete rules and answers; lifted mechanical solidarity shares rules that generate, select, apply, and revise concrete answers.
- **Generative relation:** fixed, installed meta-rules combine with current knowledge or inputs and a situation to generate a binding first-order output; generator and output remain parts of one weave.
- **Jurisdictional distinction:** a lifted meta-order may govern universally at its own level while persons, offices, institutions, communities, and contracting parties remain plural and domain-limited beneath it; that pluralism is a consequence or application, not the definition of lifting.
- **Corrigibility axis:** first-order and lifted captures can each be open or closed; epistemic sovereignty, correction failure, interpretive monopoly, and jurisdictional spillover—not rivalry alone—mark pathology.
- **Package compositionality:** compatible religious, professional, familial, national, and local packages can be braided into that one complex lens.
- **Replacement under incompatibility:** an incompatible governing program must be rejected, or the incumbent weave must be unbraided sufficiently for one successor to be rebraided and installed.
- **Three-layer causal distinction:** the historical cause of a rule, its current
  affective classification, and its presently avowed reason can coincide or
  diverge and should be measured separately.
- **Formation--construction distinction:** endogenous mythology formation is the
  distributed growth and selection of cultural articulations from changing
  conditions and a substantive research path in its own right; purposive mythology
  construction is the prospective study of attempted target-state-guided
  intervention in that same ecology.
- **Intervention-hypothesis boundary:** mythology may be a privileged symbolic
  intervention surface, but it is not the whole culture-implementation stack;
  attempted purposive construction neither places the constructor outside culture
  nor guarantees the installed successor.
- **Single-slot defeat condition:** stable same-domain operation by two
  incompatible evaluators that remain independently final, affectively alive, and
  mutually ungoverned would count against strict single-slot exclusivity.
- **Phenomenological appearance:** bigOther-presence or bigOther-as-Thou—the collective or symbolic authority encountered as an objectively present Other.
- **Kantian moral-phenomenology neighbour:** the inner court, observing judge,
  *zwiefache Persönlichkeit*, *doppeltes Selbst*, and unconditional *iubeo* or
  *veto* independently articulate how one person's moral authority can confront
  the sensible person as another; Kant's autonomous-rational ontology remains
  distinct from the local-bigOther hypothesis.
- **Agentified enactment depth:** representation, consultation or service,
  presence as Thou, distributed enactment, and fused enactment must not be
  collapsed; only the final relation entails authorial displacement.
- **Immediate target:** the elephant—the intuitive, motivational, largely nonconscious mind.
- **Conscious interface:** the rider and the main CPU.
- **Shared communication interface:** Lacan's big Other, functionally translated as the on-the-wire symbolic protocol.
- **Network operation:** reciprocal signalling through the protocol and possible synchronization.
- **Field coherence:** mechanical solidarity.
- **Emergent network state:** the distributed bigOther.
- **Self-like representation:** a multi-mind-self presentation can organize the
  collective as one represented, perceived, addressed, or incarnated self without
  establishing agency.
- **Possible collective agent:** the operative multi-mind-self or multi-mind-self proper when integration and continuity pass the relevant threshold.
- **Family-level shorthand:** unqualified *multi-mind-self* may be used
  explanatorily where no threshold inference depends upon it; claims of intention,
  ownership, responsibility, or fusion require the qualified term.
- **Nested authorship:** a network-level operative multi-mind-self can add an operative
  *we* while differentiated participating *I*s retain legitimate personal and
  subordinate jurisdictions; it does not install a second local bigOther.
- **Episodic multi-mind-self:** a distributed bigOther that crosses the
  collective-agent threshold only during bounded high-integration periods; viable
  latency, de-animation, and cultural loss remain different inter-episode states.
- **Differentiated unity:** a real shared *we* that preserves distinct persons,
  functions, judgments, relationships, and legitimate subordinate jurisdictions;
  **person-preserving unity** emphasizes this boundary.
- **Subjecthood absorption (fusion):** the independent extent to which a
  collective program denies personal judgment, conscience, purpose, property,
  loyalty, identity, relationships, refusal, or exit legitimate standing against
  the collective.
- **Fusion pressure:** dynamics that generalize compulsory alignment, subordinate
  differentiated jurisdictions, and weaken correction, refusal, exit, and appeal.
- **Total fusion:** the limiting combination of high subjecthood absorption,
  comprehensive jurisdiction, and closed correction or exit.
- **Fusion ideology:** a program that construes separate persons as alienated or
  falsely conscious fragments to be reconstituted as organs of one collective
  subject.
- **Humanity-inversion boundary:** equal human standing and an open aspirational
  horizon become fusion pressure when Humanity is agentified, assigned a true
  interest, and used to authorize privileged interpreters and sacrifice.
- **Long-term result:** reproduction or transformation of culture through ritual and institutionalization.
- **Central normative problem:** whether the bigOther remains contestable by reflective judgment or acquires final authority over it.
- **Developmental axis:** externally enforced → socially authored → self-authored → self-transforming bigOther.
- **Reflective-role bridge:** press secretary → interpreter → auditor distinguishes
  defence of a verdict from making its programming object and genuinely testing
  it; self-authorship requires more than articulate post-hoc reasons.

## Compact formulas

Single-slot composition and replacement:

> **one PCI slot → one local bigOther → one operative mythological lens; compatible packages → one braided program; incompatible governing worldview → rejection or incumbent unbraiding → liminal interval → one rebraided successor**

Historical explanation and present execution:

> **historical cause ≠ live affective classification ≠ avowed reason; present
> agreement among them is an empirical question**

Formation, intervention, and selection:

> **endogenous formation: changing conditions → mismatch → articulation →
> distributed selection → embodiment; prospective purposive construction:
> candidate chosen destination D* → diagnosis → symbolic and institutional
> intervention → testing and correction; where intervention is attempted, the
> actual successor is jointly constrained by intention, receivers, incumbent and
> rival fields, institutions, consequences, and reality**

Situated scripts and unraveling:

> **operative cultural weave + role/institution/situation + practical environment
> → situated cultural script → enactment and consequence → reinforcement, repair,
> or unraveling; peripheral failure → local script replacement, while
> load-bearing cross-script cascade → possible cultural unbraiding**

Relevant-structure appraisal:

> **felt relevance × represented support or damage → possible moral appraisal and
> mobilization; actual multidimensional effect + generative centrality +
> normative standing → separate evaluation**

Sublimation-bearing symbols and lens programming:

> **carrier or activation device → installed sublimation-related mappings →
> local-bigOther classification; repeated rival mythology + systems + rewards +
> sanctions + conduct → possible lens reprogramming → changed Beseelung,
> enactment, and transmission**

Change-indexed moral mobilization:

> **represented change, continuation, prevention, or culpable omission affecting
> felt-relevant structure → intensified moral classification and action readiness
> (hypothesis, not definition)**

Single-slot hard test:

> **two incompatible same-domain evaluators + independent finality + independent
> affect, veto, and correction + stable coexistence without hierarchy,
> reinterpretation, contextual division, or third rule → evidence against strict
> single-slot exclusivity**

Order, jurisdiction, and lifting:

> **one fully occupied slot × {shared concrete rules and answers | shared answer-generating meta-rules} × {corrigible | pathological}; fixed M + current K_t + situation C → binding R_t(C); lifting may enable plural first-order outputs and bounded jurisdictions without creating a second bigOther**

Person--collective constitution:

> **collective agency × subjecthood absorption × jurisdiction × corrigibility;
> differentiated unity preserves personal and subordinate standing, while
> generalized compulsory alignment + jurisdictional absorption + blocked
> correction, refusal, and exit → fusion pressure → possible total fusion**

Agentified enactment and the Jaynes--Bach bridge:

> **mythology → god-agent represented in one operative lens → agentified
> *Beseelung* / bigOther-as-Thou → distributed enactment → multi-mind-self
> presentation → possible operative multi-mind-self; displacement of personal
> authorship within that agent → fused multi-mind-self**

Kantian inner-tribunal comparison:

> **one numerically identical human + distinct practical positions → inner moral
> authority represented as observing and judging other; Kant: autonomous
> practical reason and doubled personality | Culture Mechanics: possible
> local-bigOther presence; shared phenomenological target ≠ shared ontology**

Conformity and targeting signals, moralization, and enforcement--exclusion:

> **ideological program → performed, omitted, imposed, or attributed token →
> conformity, refusal, or target status becomes observable → character disclosure
> and accusation → distributed judgment → enforcement through inclusion,
> exclusion, coercion, or violence → defence or expansion of jurisdiction**

Rivalry and conflict:

> **propositional, causal-story, or classification conflict → possible
> mythological rivalry → active interference with transmission, authority,
> membership, or institutional embodiment → mythological conflict → organized
> recruitment and rival-signal suppression → ideological-form conflict → physical
> coercion or violence → fundamentalist-form conflict**

Full culture-mechanical cycle:

> **mythologies shape the mythological lens → the local bigOther maps observations onto social feelings → the elephant moves → the rider explains or questions → behaviour is encoded in the shared symbolic protocol → other local bigOthers classify and respond → an emergent field acts back upon its participants → distributed synchronization reproduces or transforms culture**
